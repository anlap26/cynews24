Erhurman puts the odds of a Guterres trilateral at "51 to 49"

On Tuesday he said he was selling neither false hope nor despair, and that he expected "something new" to emerge. A trilateral, he said, means negotiating with the Turkish Cypriots, not with Turkey.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Turkish Cypriot leader Tufan Erhurman said on Tuesday that he is "51-per-cent" optimistic there will be a result to announce after his planned trilateral with President Nikos Christodoulides and UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres. The meeting is expected later this month. "I do not like selling false hope, nor do I like selling despair. Let's say 51 to 49. I think something new will emerge," the Cyprus Mail quotes him telling Genc TV. He added that Guterres "would probably not want to hold his last Cyprus meeting in vain": the secretary-general's term ends in six weeks.

Erhurman insisted the meeting should not include the guarantor powers, Greece, Turkey and Britain. Earlier enlarged meetings with those guarantors, where only confidence-building measures were discussed, were "a game of putting the Greek Cypriot side and Turkey on equal footing, and excluding the Turkish Cypriots". "A trilateral means you are negotiating with us, not with Turkey."

Asked about UN envoy Maria Angela Holguin's remark that "what Tufan calls methodology is not a methodology" — a reference to the four points he says must be met before negotiations start — Erhurman said Holguin had, in the same interview, suggested starting with a rotating presidency. She had called that question both "the one thing which can block any effort" and "fundamental". He said "the process will not progress if there is no progress on this issue". "Even if she does not call it methodology, that is not my problem. You can place it wherever you want, but you know that this is the essence of the matter. It cannot be done without this." Elements of his methodology, he said, have been "internalised by the UN": a transitional period in which parts of a solution are applied as they are agreed. That, he said, matches the fourth of his four points. "If the Greek Cypriots say no again, we will have preserved the rights we gained during the transitional period. We will not return to the current status quo."

On Christodoulides's 19 points he said "we used it when we found reasonable things in it", and that his main objection was timing: the package arrived days before the UN General Assembly in September. "The UN had put forward seven points which it had distilled from previous discussions. We had been talking for eleven months, and then suddenly you bring a 19-point package." Cyprus Times, citing the Turkish Cypriot press, carries the same "51 to 49" and adds that he calls political equality the first point of his methodology, that a rotating presidency should not be left to the final phase, and that without political equality, he said, there can be no settlement and Turkish Cypriots will not accept minority status. Decisions on the Cyprus problem, he said there, are taken in consultation with Ankara.

On crossing points he rejected the idea that Mia Milia was "a request from the Turkish side". "When Mia Milia opens, will Greek Cypriots not cross? Do only Turkish Cypriots cross from Ayios Dhometios?" The road linking Athienou and Aglandjia, he said, "is actually needed by Greek Cypriots", but he does not judge crossings by "who will benefit". "It does not bother me if the lives of Greek Cypriots are made easier. The agreement for Kampos and Louroujina does not mean giving up on Mia Milia. Mia Milia is on the table and is the number one item on the agenda of the tripartite meeting. It is not a Turkish crossing. It is a Turkish and Greek Cypriot crossing." Of the proposal he called game-changing and gave Christodoulides last week, he said the fact it had not leaked suggested it might have a chance. "If it were a proposal they could easily reject, it would have been leaked long ago and they would have tried to turn it into a rag. Since it has not been leaked, it means the proposal has legs."
