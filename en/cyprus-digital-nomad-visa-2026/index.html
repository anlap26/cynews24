Cyprus digital nomad visa in 2026: who it is for

The Migration Department is accepting applications again, with a cap of 500. The income test is €3,500 net a month. The permit is not a job in Cyprus and not, by itself, a GeSY card.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

The phrase "digital nomad visa" sounds like a product. On the Migration Department's pages it is a temporary residence scheme for people who already earn a living somewhere else. The Department says it is again accepting applications. The maximum number of permits that can be granted remains 500. This guide uses the Department's pages and its document list dated 19 March 2025. If those pages move, they win.

Who it is for

The scheme is for nationals of countries outside the EU and outside the EEA who can do their work from anywhere with telecommunications. They must be employed by a company registered abroad, and able to work without being tied to a place, or be self-employed and provide services remotely to clients located abroad. The work must not be activity for an employer registered in Cyprus, and it must not be services to a client established in Cyprus. An affirmation to that effect is on the document list. A person who wants a Cyprus job needs a different permit. The routes are separated in EU residence in Cyprus.

EU and EEA nationals are not the audience of this scheme. They use the registration route for Union citizens, not this file.

The income figure, and what is added for a family

The Department asks for stable and sufficient monthly net income of at least €3,500, after contributions and tax. The document list says the amount is increased by 20 percent for a spouse or partner and by 15 percent for each child. Where the papers do not already show the net figure, the list asks for an affirmation that the net income is not below the minimum. It also asks for evidence of salary and for detailed bank statements of the last six months showing stable income from outside the Republic.

This page does not convert €3,500 into a budget for Limassol or Paphos. No official source used here publishes one monthly cost of living. What the island does and does not publish is in the cost of living guide.

How the permit is described

The Department's benefits list says the holder may reside for a year, with a possibility of renewal for a further two years. The first temporary residence permit, it says, is issued for one year. Family members, meaning a spouse or civil-union partner and minor children, may reside for the same period and may not work or carry out an economic activity in Cyprus.

The fee printed for issue or renewal of the temporary residence permit is €70. The Department says examination takes between five and seven weeks. An applicant who arrives for this purpose is told to submit the application, with the documents on the list, within three months of arrival, at the Department's central offices in Nicosia. Documents are to be officially translated and certified. An incomplete file, the page says, is not accepted. A person already legally in Cyprus under another status may apply to switch. Applications can be made in person or through an authorised representative.

The list also asks for a passport valid at least three months beyond the application, the page showing the last entry, a criminal-record certificate, medical certificates, health insurance for inpatient and outpatient care and transportation of a corpse (the list calls this Plan A), and a title deed or a stamped rental agreement plus a utility bill. The medical lines are specific and they can change. Read the current PDF rather than a blog that repeats an older list. This page is not a medical instruction.

Tax and health are not printed on the card

The Department's page says that a person who resides in the Republic for one or more periods totalling more than 183 days in the same tax year is a Cyprus tax resident, provided they are not a tax resident of any other country. That sentence is the migration page's summary. The Tax Department's own page is the residence test, and from 1 January 2026 the 60-day rule no longer requires that the person is not tax resident elsewhere. The two pages should be read together, not averaged. The test, the bands and what non-dom status does and does not do are in Cyprus tax residency in 2026 and the non-dom guide.

Health insurance on the document list is a private policy that meets the list. It is not enrolment in GeSY. Who GeSY actually covers is in GeSY for foreigners. Questions readers ask

Who can apply?

The Migration Department says the scheme is for non-EU and non-EEA nationals who work remotely, with technology, for an employer registered abroad or for clients abroad. Work for a Cyprus employer or a Cyprus client is outside the scheme.

What income is required?

Stable net monthly income of at least €3,500 after contributions and tax. The Department's document list raises that by 20 percent for a spouse or partner and by 15 percent for each child. The proof it asks for includes pay evidence and six months of bank statements.

How long is the permit, and how many are issued?

The Department says the first temporary residence permit is for one year, with a possibility of renewal for a further two years, and that the maximum number of permits remains 500. The fee it prints for issue or renewal is €70. It says examination takes five to seven weeks.

Can my family work?

No. The Department says a spouse or partner and minor children may reside for the same period, without the right to be employed or to carry out an economic activity in Cyprus.

Does the permit make me tax resident or a GeSY beneficiary?

No. Tax residence is a separate test, explained in the tax residency guide. The Department's own tax sentence should be read next to the Tax Department page, because the 60-day rule changed in 2026. GeSY enrolment is a third question and is not printed on the nomad permit.
