Best things to do in Cyprus in 2026, city by city

Things to do in Cyprus in 2026 by city: Nicosia, Limassol, Paphos, Larnaca, Ayia Napa and the Troodos, from the Deputy Ministry of Tourism. Beaches are public, and more than 75 fly a Blue Flag.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 19 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 19 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Cyprus is the third largest island in the Mediterranean, 9,251 square kilometres, a republic and a member of the European Union. That is how the Deputy Ministry of Tourism opens its 2026 guide. What follows is not this newsroom’s ranking of “best” sights. It is the route the ministry itself sets out, day by day and city by city. It calls the short distances one of the practical facts of the island: from a resort or a village you can plan a day without another flight.

Nicosia

Lefkosia is the capital and the business centre, a city inside Venetian walls. Eleftheria Square, the guide says, joins the old city to the new one, and was designed by Zaha Hadid Architects. The ministry’s morning list is the Cyprus Handicraft Centre, the Cyprus Archaeological Museum, the Byzantine Museum, galleries and St John’s Cathedral. Lunch is in Laiki Geitonia, a restored quarter of cafés and shops. The afternoon is the Hadjigeorgakis Kornesios Ethnological Museum and the Leventis Municipal Museum.

Limassol

Lemesos is the island’s biggest port, between ancient Amathus to the east and ancient Kourion to the west. The ministry notes the carnival, the winemaking villages in the hills, and the newer towers on the seafront. Its sample day is Kolossi Castle, from the 13th century, then Kourion and the Sanctuary of Apollo Ylatis, and after lunch the medieval castle and Amathous. The same booklet describes Kourion as a settlement destroyed by earthquake in the 4th century AD: mosaic houses, a Greco-Roman theatre and an agora on the cliff. Kolossi later housed the Knights of St John, who are tied in the text to Commandaria wine.

Lady’s Mile is called the longest beach on the island: shallow water and dark volcanic sand. All beaches in Cyprus, the ministry writes, are public and free. Showers, sunbeds and umbrellas on municipal beaches are for a fee. More than 75 beaches carry a Blue Flag. The same brochure says the island has over 300 days of sunshine a year.

Paphos

Paphos is split in the booklet into Kato Paphos by the sea and Pano Paphos above. Kato Paphos sits almost on the Hellenistic city, and the whole ancient city of Paphos is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The ministry’s day is Petra tou Romiou, the legendary birthplace of Aphrodite; the Tombs of the Kings; the Archaeological Park mosaics in the Houses of Aion, Dionysos and Theseus; then the castle and St Paul’s Pillar. The mosaics are dated from the 2nd to the 5th century. The Tombs of the Kings are rock-cut tombs of the Hellenistic and Roman periods; the name comes from their size, and the people buried there were officials and aristocracy. The next day in the booklet is Polis, the Baths of Aphrodite and the Akamas trails.

Larnaca

Larnaca’s seafront is the Finikoudes promenade, lined with palms. The booklet sets Christianity and Islam side by side: the Church of Saint Lazarus and the Hala Sultan Mosque. The morning is that church, the Pierides Museum and the city-kingdom of Kition. Lunch is on the Finikoudes. The afternoon is Choirokoitia, a Neolithic settlement and also a UNESCO site, and Lefkara, known for lace. Mackenzie Beach is described as shallow and as a centre of Larnaca nightlife.

The primary airports, in the same publication, are Larnaca (LCA) and Paphos (PFO), at hermesairports.com. Limassol port takes most cargo and cruise traffic; Larnaca port is smaller.

Ayia Napa and Protaras

The Famagusta region in the booklet means the resorts of Ayia Napa and Paralimni–Protaras, not a walk through fenced Varosha. The ministry’s day is the Thalassa museum, Ayia Napa monastery, swimming, and the trails at Cape Greco. Nissi is a bay with an islet you can walk to at low tide. Konnos is a small sandy beach, with Cape Greco National Park a little over a mile away. Fig Tree Bay in Protaras is named for a fig tree the booklet says has grown there since the 17th century.

Troodos

A separate day is Kykkos Monastery and its museum, lunch in Pedoulas, one of ten UNESCO Byzantine churches, Archangel Michael, then Agios Nikolaos tis Stegis in Kakopetria. Wine, on the archaeology in the same text, goes back almost 6,000 years. Commandaria is called the oldest appellation wine; zivania is the local spirit at 40 to 60 per cent alcohol.

Under water the booklet singles out the Zenobia wreck, which it ranks among the top ten wreck dives in the world, and the MUSAN museum: 93 sculptures by Jason deCaires Taylor. It gives an average diving temperature of 24°C and water of about 16 to 27°C.

Questions readers ask

Are Cyprus beaches free?

The Deputy Ministry of Tourism says all beaches are public and free of charge. Showers, sunbeds and umbrellas on municipal beaches are for a fee.

Which airports does the tourism guide name?

The 2026 booklet names Larnaca (LCA) and Paphos (PFO) international airports as the primary ports of entry by air.

Is all of Paphos a UNESCO site?

The booklet says the whole ancient city of Paphos is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Choirokoitia and ten Byzantine churches in the Troodos are also named as UNESCO sites.
