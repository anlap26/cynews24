HELLENiQ Energy leaves the door open to a Cyprus offshore gas partnership

Cyprus gas HELLENiQ Energy: chief executive Andreas Shiamishis said a future role through a partner is still under consideration, but nine Greek blocks are the priority for three to four years.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 19 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 19 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

HELLENiQ Energy has not ruled out joining Cyprus’s offshore natural-gas exploration through a partnership with another company, chief executive Andreas Shiamishis said, while the Greek group puts its existing exploration portfolio in Greece first. He was speaking to international journalists in Athens, at a gathering the Cyprus Mail attended.

He said the earlier decision to stay out of Cyprus’s exclusive economic zone was driven mainly by a lack of deepwater exploration experience and by the political and operational risks. “What we may do in the future is still under consideration and clarification,” he said. The company had concluded it could not offer the expertise Cyprus was, in its view, looking for when the offshore programme was being developed. “The decision to not get involved in Cyprus’ EEZ was taken some years ago. As a company, we felt that we could not offer what Cyprus was potentially seeking at the time. This included know-how in deep exploration, as the reserves are located in deep waters.” At that time, he said, HELLENiQ Energy had neither produced oil nor drilled, so expressing a wish to take part “would have been irresponsible”.

A second factor, he said, is that offshore work in the region needs a political stability that, in his view, “can only be provided by the largest international companies”. Cyprus Mail noted that the island’s programme has involved ExxonMobil, Eni, TotalEnergies and Chevron. HELLENiQ Energy already supplies and markets liquid fuels, lubricants and liquefied petroleum gas in Cyprus, and it has renewable-energy activity there. That presence has not extended to offshore hydrocarbon exploration.

Asked about further licensing rounds, Shiamishis pointed to nine blocks in Greek waters that “have to be developed”. The company has brought in international partners, including ExxonMobil and Chevron, and kept minority stakes. “We have a lot of work to do in Greece over the next three to four years. I am not excluding the possibility of a potential collaboration with another company in Cyprus’ blocks. However, at the current moment, this is not our first priority.” He said he hoped Cyprus would do well, calling it “a 20-year journey”.

In February 2026, the paper reported, HELLENiQ Energy and Chevron secured exploration rights for four offshore areas south of Crete and the Peloponnese. Chevron holds 70 per cent as operator and HELLENiQ Energy 30 per cent. The four areas cover about 47,000 square kilometres, with some targets in water deeper than 1,500 metres. That is a Greek licence, not a Cyprus block.

The company’s 2025 annual report, as cited by the paper, put installed renewable capacity at the end of that year at 494 megawatts, including 41 megawatts of photovoltaic capacity in Cyprus, and a pipeline that included 41 megawatts of hybrid projects in Cyprus. None of those figures is an offshore gas discovery. A separate count of Greek direct investment on the island is here.
