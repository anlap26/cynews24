GeSY for foreigners: who is a beneficiary, and what the contributions are

Being in Cyprus is not the same as being in GeSY. The Health Insurance Organisation lists categories. A visitor, and a non-EU holder of only a temporary permit, should not assume the list includes them. The full contribution rates have applied since 1 March 2020.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

GeSY, the General Health System, is the public scheme run with the Health Insurance Organisation. The question foreigners actually have is not what the letters stand for. It is whether they are a beneficiary. The HIO's own beneficiary page is the list. This guide does not add a category the page does not contain.

The categories, in the HIO's words

Cypriot citizens who are ordinary residents of the areas the Republic controls, or of the Sovereign Base Areas, are one category. EU citizens who are ordinary residents of the areas the Republic controls and who either work there, or hold a permanent residence permit under the law on free movement, are another. Non-EU citizens who are ordinary residents and who either hold a permanent residence permit, or have the right of equal treatment in social insurance under the aliens and immigration law, are another. Refugees and people with subsidiary protection, ordinarily resident, are another. Family members are listed with their own conditions. For family members of a non-EU beneficiary who holds a permanent residence permit, the HIO says the members must also hold a permanent residence permit.

A visitor is not on that list. A person who has just arrived and has not finished a residence file is not on it by virtue of the landing. The site's relocation guide puts GeSY after the migration record can identify a non-Cypriot who holds a valid residence permit. The resident handbook separates a beneficiary record from a place on a personal doctor's list. This page does not repeat those steps as if they were optional.

The temporary permit, including the nomad card

A digital nomad permit is a temporary residence permit for a year, as the Migration Department describes it. The HIO's non-EU category speaks of permanent residence or equal treatment in social insurance. Those are not the same words as "temporary permit". This guide will not bridge the gap with a guess. If you hold only the nomad card, ask the HIO whether any category applies, and keep the answer. Until then, the insurance the nomad file itself asks for is a private policy. The difference between that policy, a travel policy and GeSY is the health insurance guide.

What people pay

The HIO financing page sets the rates. Full rates have applied since 1 March 2020. An employee pays 2.65 percent of earnings. An employer pays 2.90 percent. The state pays 4.70 percent on the earnings, self-employed remuneration, officials' pay and pensions it lists. A self-employed person pays 4 percent. A pensioner pays 2.65 percent of the pension. An income earner, the page's examples include rent, interest and dividends, pays 2.65 percent. An official pays 2.65 percent. The person who pays an official's remuneration pays 2.90 percent.

The maximum total annual amount on which contributions are paid, for each natural person, is €180,000. The HIO says a person who is not a Cyprus tax resident pays contributions only on income, earnings and pensions that arise in the Republic, excluding dividends and interest. That sentence is about contributions. It is not a statement that the person is a beneficiary.

Earlier rates, from the first phase in 2019, still circulate. They are the lower percentages the HIO table shows for 1 March 2019 to 29 February 2020. They are not the rates in force after 1 March 2020.

What to do in a gap

For the months before any public enrolment, a private policy is the document that is actually in force, if you bought one. This page does not name a clinic that will bill a given insurer. Emergency services are on 112.

Contributions are one line in a budget, not the budget. The cost of living guide keeps that line separate from rent.

Questions readers ask

Am I covered because I live in Cyprus?

No. The HIO's beneficiary page requires ordinary residence plus a category: for example citizenship, or an EU citizen who works or holds a permanent residence document, or a non-EU citizen with a permanent residence permit or equal treatment in social insurance. Read the category that matches you. A stay is not a category.

What are the rates?

On the HIO financing page the full rates, since 1 March 2020, are 2.65 percent for an employee, 2.90 percent for an employer, 4.70 percent for the state, 4 percent for a self-employed person, and 2.65 percent for a pensioner, an income earner and an official. The maximum annual amount contributions are paid on is €180,000.

Does a digital nomad permit enrol me?

The nomad permit is a temporary residence permit. The HIO's non-EU category, as published, speaks of a permanent residence permit or equal treatment in social insurance. This page does not treat the nomad card as a GeSY card. Ask the HIO.

Does GeSY cover me abroad?

GeSY is the national system. Care outside Cyprus is not what it is for. Travel and longer-stay private policies are a different product, set out in the international health insurance guide.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is a reading of two HIO pages. Emergencies in the EU, Cyprus included, are reached on 112. A policy does not replace that call.
