Buying property in Cyprus as a foreigner: the permission, and what it is not

Citizens of non-EU countries need permission before the transfer. The Ministry of Interior describes the limits and says the application itself has no fee. EU and EEA buyers are treated differently. A contract is not a title deed.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

People searching "buy property in Cyprus as a foreigner" usually want a yes or a no. The law is a permission, with a limit, for some buyers, and no permission for others. The page this guide treats as current is the Ministry of Interior's page on purchasing property. The statute is the Immovable Property Acquisition (Aliens) Law, Cap. 109. An English consolidation from the Law Commissioner stops at amendments to 2007 and still describes an older residence test. The Greek text on CyLaw is the later consolidation. Where they differ, do not prefer the older English file.

Who needs the permit

The Ministry says foreign nationals, which it defines as citizens of non-EU member states, including foreign companies and companies controlled by foreign nationals as the law defines them, must obtain permission before they acquire immovable property. The application is form COMM 145, filed at the District Administration of the district where the property is, with the supporting papers the page lists. The Ministry says no fee is payable for the permission and that applications take between two and three weeks.

The Ministry's prerequisites are specific. A foreign national or a foreign couple can be permitted to buy a plot, or land not divided into plots, of up to 4,000 square metres, to build a dwelling for the owner to live in. Or they can be permitted up to two units, which may be at different stages. Those units may be two dwellings, or one dwelling and a shop of up to 100 square metres, or one dwelling and an office of up to 250 square metres. The restriction applies to the couple together. Couples are granted joint permission.

Cap. 109 says no alien acquires immovable property, other than by death, without the permit of the Council of Ministers first obtained. It also says a contract does not give the alien the right to acquire the property until that permit is granted. Paying a deposit on a contract that still needs a permit is not the same as owning the flat. The papers the Ministry lists include the contract of sale with stamp duty, a copy of the title, plans, evidence of means, passports, and, where there is no separate title yet, the planning consent. A company buyer has a further list. Read the live page. This guide does not reprint every attachment.

EU and EEA buyers

The Ministry's page frames the permission around citizens of non-EU states. The Greek consolidation of Cap. 109 on CyLaw excludes from the definition of alien a citizen of an EU member state under the treaty, and a citizen of an EEA contracting state. That is a citizenship test, not a test of where the buyer currently sleeps. The 2007 English consolidation still talks about permanent residence and a secondary home. It is the wrong file to rely on for a 2026 purchase. If a seller, an agent or an old printout describes the residence test as current, ask the District Administration which text it is applying. This page will not resolve a dispute between an agent and the statute.

Tax on the purchase is not the permit

The permit is not a tax assessment. PwC's Tax Facts and Figures 2026 states transfer fees of 3 percent on the first €85,000, 5 percent from €85,001 to €170,000 and 8 percent above that, then says no transfer fee is payable if VAT applies on the purchase, and that the fees are reduced by 50 percent if VAT does not apply. Those sentences are in a booklet, not on a Land Registry receipt. The Department of Lands and Surveys is the office that charges the fee. Confirm the figure there before completion.

Value-added tax on a new home, including any reduced rate for a first residence, depends on conditions the Tax Department publishes. This guide does not copy a square-metre cap or a rate it has not set beside the current notice. Stamp duty on the contract is similarly a Tax Department matter. None of these is folded into a "total cost of buying" on this page, because the total depends on whether the home is new, whether VAT applies and whether a permit is needed.

Title, and the pages around this one

A title deed is the Land Registry record. A contract of sale is not that record. This page does not know, and does not invent, how many homes are waiting for a separate title. Ask for the title copy the Ministry itself lists as a supporting document, and ask what is missing if the answer is that a separate title is not issued yet.

Renting instead of buying is renting in Cyprus. The running cost after you have keys is not a transfer fee; it is the subject of the cost of living guide. Nothing here is a valuation or a statement that a particular project is a sound purchase. Notes on named developments, where this site has reported what the developer publishes, are collected on the guides hub.

Questions readers ask

Do EU citizens need permission to buy?

The Ministry of Interior's current page requires permission for citizens of non-EU states, including companies it defines as foreign-controlled. The Greek text of Cap. 109 on CyLaw also places EU citizens and EEA citizens outside the definition of alien. An old English consolidation still describes an earlier residence test. If you are not sure which text applies to you, ask the District Administration before you pay a deposit.

What can a non-EU buyer be permitted to acquire?

The Ministry's page says a plot or undivided land up to 4,000 square metres for a dwelling the owner will live in, or up to two units. The units may be two dwellings, or one dwelling and a shop up to 100 square metres, or one dwelling and an office up to 250 square metres. Couples share the limit and receive joint permission.

How do you apply, and what does it cost?

Form COMM 145 goes to the District Administration where the property is. The Ministry says no fee is payable for the permission and that applications take between two and three weeks. The contract of sale does not, under Cap. 109, give the buyer the right to acquire the property until the permit is granted.

Are transfer fees and VAT decided on this page?

No. PwC's 2026 booklet states a scale and says transfer fees are not payable when VAT applies, and are reduced by 50 percent when VAT does not. Those are PwC's statements. The Department of Lands and Surveys is where the fee is actually charged. A reduced VAT rate for a first home has conditions this page does not copy.

Is a signed contract the same as ownership?

No. Title is a Land Registry matter. A contract, a permit and a title deed are three documents. This page does not estimate how long a separate title takes.
