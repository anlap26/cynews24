Hundreds of graves at Saints Constantine and Helen were sold more than once, a church official estimates

Andreas Karayiorgis, secretary of the cemetery church committee, tells Phileleftheros of 700 to 750 repeat sales. Archbishop Georgios says he will act if the complaints hold. Karayiorgis himself calls one 2003 receipt a forgery.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Phileleftheros has gone back to old grave sales at the Saints Constantine and Helen cemetery in Pallouriotissa. Andreas Karayiorgis, secretary of the church committee of the church inside the cemetery, told the paper that graves sold more than once may number 700 to 750, and that the takings of those who sold them again run to hundreds of thousands of euros. The dearest, he said, went for 15,000 Cyprus pounds, others for 6,000 to 11,000 pounds. The sales came in two waves and from different people: one charged up to 300 pounds, the other asked thousands for each grave.

The paper sets out a complaint from Andreas Ionas. One morning he arrived at the cemetery and found part of the family grave opened for a burial from another family. He did not stop the funeral. He later found that the extension had also been sold to someone else, a person he says he has no quarrel with: that buyer, he writes, evidently purchased the plot without knowing it already belonged to a family.

In his letter to the paper, Ionas says the main plot was bought in 1961. He produced a receipt headed “Greek Cemetery of Nicosia”, with the words “purchase of a space”, measuring 9.5 by 7 feet, and a treasurer’s signature on a Republic stamp. In 2003 his mother bought the neighbouring corner, 9 by 2 feet, for 150 pounds, as an extension of square 30. Ionas writes that the cemetery told him this extension had been sold five years ago to the person who recently buried a relative there. The day before that funeral, he says, there was no sign of a sale. When his father died this February, Ionas asked to bury him on the extension, but was told to bury him with the rest of the family and that the priest of the time had not entered the sale in the book. Ionas points to the priest’s signature and an archbishopric receipt.

Archbishop Georgios, whom the paper contacted, said he hoped this was not a new case but one of those examined before he was elected. “If the complaints hold, I will be relentless,” Phileleftheros reports him saying. He asked anyone who has met the same thing, or who believes they were taken advantage of, to bring documents.

Karayiorgis was shown the complaint and the papers. He told the paper he is sure the 2003 title is forged and that the complainant was deceived. The article does not close the matter: the archbishop is asking for evidence, and the committee secretary disputes one of the receipts.
