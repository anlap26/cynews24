Christodoulides summoned as witness in occupied-property usurpation trial

Lawyers for German national Ewa Isabella Kunzel, who is on trial in Nicosia on 46 charges linked to property in the north, confirmed on Thursday that a witness summons has been issued to the president. They want him to say that bringing such cases to court is a first-time government policy, and argue that the dispute is political rather than legal.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 26 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 26 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

President Nikos Christodoulides has been summoned as a witness in the trial of German national Ewa Isabella Kunzel, who is accused of usurping Greek Cypriot property in the occupied north. Her lawyers, Sotiris Argyrou & Associates, confirmed on Thursday that a summons had been issued in the case before the Nicosia criminal court.

The defence wants the president to testify “that it is government policy to bring such cases before the courts, a policy being pursued for the first time by any government”. That, the firm said, forms part of its argument that the issue is political rather than legal. Trying to shift “a clearly political issue onto the shoulders of the Cypriot courts”, the statement added, “makes it impossible for a fair trial to be conducted by local courts, thereby leaving the justice system exposed”. The lawyers said they were speaking after leaks to the media and would make no further comment while the case is under way.

Kunzel faces 46 charges, including fraudulent property transactions, unlawful possession and use of property without the owners’ consent, and money laundering. She has been in custody since her arrest at Larnaca airport in July 2024, after a conversation on a flight with Elam MEP Geadis Geadi in which, prosecutors say, she admitted selling Greek Cypriot-owned homes in the north.

In September the court of appeal unanimously rejected her latest request for release. Her lawyer, Sotiris Argyrou, has previously argued that the prosecution is politically motivated, and has said Kunzel turned down a plea bargain because she would not admit an offence she denies committing. In May the Nicosia criminal court ruled that European investigation orders used to gather evidence in Germany had been lawfully issued, but that the search of her luggage and the seizure of items had breached her constitutional rights and were inadmissible. The defence statement did not say whether the summons has already been served, or when the president might be called.
