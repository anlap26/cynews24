No Cyprus internet cut is scheduled for 17 November; the global blackout claim is false

Viral warnings of a power, internet and banking blackout do not match any Cypriot provider notice. The dated event is a short US radio and television alert test.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

By Tuesday morning no Cypriot internet provider had announced a disconnection: the reporting names no company, no districts and no time at which the island’s connection would be switched off. Phileleftheros put the claim on the page as a new conspiracy theory, “a global blackout today”. The English-language Philenews states outright that viral warnings of a loss of electricity, the internet and banking on Tuesday 17 November are false.

What is actually on the calendar is set out by the US Federal Emergency Management Agency. With the Federal Communications Commission, FEMA is running a nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System at approximately 2:20 p.m. Eastern Time on Tuesday 17 November 2026, which is 9:20 p.m. in Cyprus. US radio and television programming will be interrupted briefly. FEMA says this is only a test and that no action is required. It is not an operation on Cypriot networks, and it is not a switch for the internet.

Philenews and a PolitiFact check describe the test as lasting about one minute. PolitiFact adds that it airs on radio and television and is not sent to mobile phones. The FEMA page used here speaks of a brief break in radio and television and does not describe a power or connectivity cut.

The “up to 90 days” figure repeated in the videos is not tied to Cyprus. Philenews, citing the Texas broadcaster KBTX, says it surfaced after US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth presented a preparedness plan on 30 September, including the FORTRESS America initiative: military departments and defence agencies should be able to operate on their own for at least 90 days, with water, electricity, fuel and communications. That is a backup plan for bases, not an announcement of a blackout. Another strand points to The Simpsons episode “It’s a Blunderful Life”, first shown on Fox on 19 November 2023. Philenews says the episode contains no documented reference to November 2026.

Russian-language versions go further, talking of a “digital apocalypse”, bank hacks and wiped accounts. Philenews treats those as part of the same unconfirmed retelling. The paper notes that similar scares were examined earlier this year, including a nine-day global blackout said to start in July and a two-minute worldwide cut on 12 August. Fact-checkers did not confirm them.

Cyber attacks and grid failures do happen, and they hit particular areas or countries. That is not the same as a collapse booked for 17 November. On this morning’s sources, a Cyprus outage still has no provider, no map and no start time.
