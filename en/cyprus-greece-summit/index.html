Christodoulides and Mitsotakis open the fourth Cyprus–Greece summit in Nicosia

Artificial intelligence, civil protection and the passage from the Cypriot to the Greek EU presidency lead Wednesday's agenda. The spokesman put 24 ministers and deputy ministers in the two delegations.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 18 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 18 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

On Wednesday, 18 November, President Nikos Christodoulides and Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis chair the fourth Cyprus–Greece intergovernmental summit in Nicosia. Government spokesman Konstantinos Letymbiotis said on Tuesday that 24 ministers and deputy ministers would take part besides the two leaders: 11 from Cyprus and 13 from Greece. RIK reported the same split. It said Mitsotakis was due at the Presidential Palace at 11:00 for a private meeting, with the leaders' statements to the press set for 12:30.

Cyprus Inform, citing the Press and Information Office, said the day also includes bilateral ministerial meetings, a plenary session, memoranda and agreements, a joint declaration of priorities and a press conference in the palace events hall. Letymbiotis put artificial intelligence at the centre: using it across government, swapping practice, and looking at cooperation in foreign policy, civil protection, justice, health, education, labour, shipping, tourism, culture and digital government. He tied the timing to the cabinet's approval, days earlier, of the National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2026–2032.

Civil protection is the other main block. The spokesman said it would cover disaster prevention, readiness, critical infrastructure and new technology, and noted that on Thursday the House continues its debate on the bills that would reform Cyprus's civil protection system. A third strand is the European calendar. Cyprus holds the Council presidency in the first half of 2026 and Greece in the second half of 2027. He called the Cypriot presidency successful and said that experience would be passed on in the ministerial meetings.

The summit format was agreed with Mitsotakis in March 2023, on the president's initiative. Letymbiotis listed results so far: the Digital Citizen app, which he said has more than 170,000 users, and its link to the Greek app; agreements on transplants; mutual recognition of degrees; and a decision to set up a long-term planning unit at the presidency. Asked whether the two leaders would discuss delimiting maritime zones between Cyprus and Greece, he said it was not on Wednesday's agenda, that Nicosia's position was already public, and that decisions would be taken in close coordination with Athens. He rejected the suggestion of a "phobic syndrome". Work on the electricity interconnection, he said, was proceeding on the roadmap and its progress was expected to be discussed.

Kathimerini reports that the Great Sea Interconnector is drawing particular attention after seabed surveys resumed, a step both capitals welcomed. The paper describes the cable as a way to end Cyprus's electricity isolation from the European grid, with Turkey's objections and the maritime disputes still in the background. It says Greek Foreign Minister George Gerapetritis, meeting Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan on Monday for the first time in three months, pressed for the link to be finished without interruption, called it a project of European interest, and repeated that the Turkish-Libyan maritime memorandum is contrary to international law and has no legal effect.
