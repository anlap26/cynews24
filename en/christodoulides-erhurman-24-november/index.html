Christodoulides and Erhurman to meet in the Nicosia buffer zone on 24 November

Cyprus talks 24 November: the leaders meet at 15:00. A tripartite with Guterres may come before the end of the month, and an enlarged meeting in early December is still described as possible.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 19 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 19 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

President Nikos Christodoulides and Turkish Cypriot leader Tufan Erhurman will meet again on Tuesday 24 November at 15:00. Politis reported the date and time on Thursday from government spokesman Konstantinos Letymbiotis. Cyprus Mail carried the same appointment from Erhurman’s office: the meeting is in the buffer zone west of Nicosia. Preparations continue for a tripartite of the two leaders with United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres.

Cyprus Mail wrote that sources had said last week the tripartite may take place before the end of the month and could open the way to an enlarged meeting. The paper understands it will come after Guterres returns from the Philippines, where he attended the Association of Southeast Asian Nations leaders’ summit until Wednesday. It is then most likely to be held in Europe.

On his visit to the island in July, Guterres said an enlarged meeting — the two sides, the three guarantor powers Greece, Turkey and the United Kingdom, and the UN — would be convened “after adequate progress” on confidence-building measures, the meeting’s methodology and the substance of the talks. The paper was told that if that progress is judged adequate at the end of the tripartite, an enlarged meeting in the first half of December remains possible. No date for either meeting has been announced.

Letymbiotis said on Tuesday that an agreement on further crossing points before the tripartite is “feasible”. From recent talks and the regular meetings of Christodoulides and Erhurman, he said, an additional announcement on the crossings at Mia Milia and at Athienou and Pyroi is feasible even before the joint meeting. Where those two stand is set out here. Last week the leaders agreed to open two other crossings, south of Louroujina, near Larnaca, and south of Karavostasi, west of Lefka. Mayors in Nicosia and Athienou were upset that their localities were passed over. Monday’s account, written before a date existed, is here.

Erhurman told Genc TV he is “51-per-cent” optimistic that the tripartite will produce something to announce. “I do not like selling false hope, nor do I like selling despair. Let’s say 51 to 49. I think something new will emerge,” he said. He added that Guterres “would probably not want to hold his last Cyprus meeting in vain”, because the secretary-general’s term draws to a close in six weeks. That 51 to 49 is his own weighing of the chances, not a forecast from the UN.
