POED and OELMEK skip the education minister's meeting on teacher appointments

The dispute is over permanent posts for about 4,000 teachers without the exam required by the 2015 law. OLTEK did attend.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 16 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 16 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

The teachers' unions POED and OELMEK did not attend a meeting on Monday 16 November with Education Minister Athena Michaelidou on the new appointment system. Reporter says the invitation went out on Thursday 12 November and the meeting was set for Monday midday. On Monday morning both unions decided not to go and sent the minister letters. RIK reports that the meeting went ahead and that OLTEK, the technical-education union, took part.

The central disagreement, Reporter says, is permanent appointment for teachers now working in schools on contract or as substitutes, without the appointment exam required by the law passed in 2015. Michaelidou, the paper says, has put their number at about 4,000. For the ministry this is a red line, because it is a legal question. The ministry had already put forward two proposals that took account of union comments, but this point remains.

In POED's letter, signed by president Myria Vasiliou and general secretary Haris Charalambous, the union says it has already set out, clearly and forcefully, its disagreement with the basic provisions of the proposal: for POED these are matters of principle and non-negotiable. Applying the proposal as it stands, the letter says, would create distortions for everyone seeking an appointment, from current candidates to the next generation of teachers, and would undermine the new system as a whole. The union asks the minister to reconsider the points in dispute in substance. A meeting, POED says both in the letter and in a statement carried by RIK, will be useful only if she first says she is willing to review those provisions. If she insists on positions which, in the letter's words, "victimise thousands of teachers already working in the public school", a dynamic response should be taken as given.

OELMEK's letter said the union would not take part in Monday's meeting on the appointment system. Its president, Konstantinos Konstantinou, told RIK that the organisation had received no answer to questions it put several weeks ago. Teachers were also angered, he said, by the unilateral decision to send the disputed bill out to public consultation. The request for a meeting with the president of the Republic, he said, still stands.
