International health insurance for people who live and work abroad: an honest breakdown

A policy for a holiday is not a policy for a year abroad. Travel medical cover, a long-stay plan, a European health card and Cyprus’s GeSY answer different questions, and none of the prices are printed here.

People say “international health insurance” and mean at least four different things. A travel medical policy is built around a trip: an accident, a sudden illness, and usually a journey home. A longer-stay international plan is built around living outside the country that issued your passport, and it may include care that is not an emergency. A European Health Insurance Card is neither of those. It is a free card from the public insurer in the country that already covers you. And a national system such as Cyprus’s GeSY is a fourth thing: the public scheme of the country where you live, once you are actually enrolled in it.

Mixing them up is how people arrive with the wrong document. A temporary-stay card does not become a residence policy because the stay got longer, and another month of a travel policy does not turn it into routine cover. This guide separates the four. It does not quote a premium, a limit or a list of clinics. Those figures change. Nothing here is a quote or a decision about GeSY enrolment.

What a travel medical policy is for

Travel medical cover is what most visitors already know. It is meant for a person away from home for a defined period who needs treatment that cannot wait. The useful questions are in the wording, not in the slogan.

The certificate should name the insurer, who may not be the company whose logo is on the website. The excess, sometimes called a deductible, and the maximum the policy will pay are numbers this page does not print, because they differ by plan and by year. Read the schedule you are offered. Many travel policies assume you have a home to return to and limit or exclude care in your country of residence, so a policy written for a tourist can be the wrong contract after you have moved, even when the marketing says “nomad”.

A condition you already have may be excluded, covered only as an acute flare-up, or offered as a paid option. Care in the United States is often capped, excluded or priced as its own tier. Routine care, a check-up, a prescription you already take, maternity and mental-health care are usually outside a classic travel policy, or inside it only as an emergency. The European Commission’s card does not pay for a flight home. A travel policy might, and it might require a call to an assistance line before you arrange anything. Some plans can be bought after you have left. Some impose a waiting period or refuse anything that started before the start date. Ask, and keep the answer.

What a longer-stay plan is for

A longer-stay international health plan, sometimes sold as expatriate or international private medical insurance, is aimed at people who live outside their home country for months or years. It may cover more than an emergency. It may also refuse the things a new arrival most wants: an operation for a problem they already have, pregnancy in the first months of the policy, or care in a country the schedule leaves out.

The honest comparison is a list of mismatches, not a star rating. If you can join the public system of the country you will live in, a private plan is a bridge or an extra, not a substitute for the enrolment rules. If you move every few months and no public system will take you, you may outgrow a travel policy. Price care in the United States on its own. If someone on the plan is pregnant or needs regular treatment, read those clauses first.

Waiting periods matter more here than on a two-week policy. So does the word “resident”. A plan can exclude the country where you have settled, or insist on a home country you can still be sent back to. If you no longer have one, say so before you pay.

The European card, and why a move is different

The European Commission’s page on the European Health Insurance Card is plain. The card is free. It lets you get medically necessary, state-provided healthcare during a temporary stay in the 27 EU countries, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland or the United Kingdom, on the same terms and at the same cost as a person insured in that country. It is not an alternative to travel insurance. It does not cover private healthcare, a flight home or lost property. It does not cover a trip whose purpose is to get treatment. It does not promise that care is free, because public systems charge different things.

The same page says that if you move and make the new country your habitual residence, you should register with an S1 form rather than rely on the card. A card from the country you left is a temporary-stay document. It is not the enrolment paper for the country you now live in. Which institution issues an S1, and whether you are entitled to one, depends on where you were insured and why you moved. This guide does not decide that. The institution that covers you at home does.

Cyprus, GeSY, and the gap before you are in

Cyprus has a national health system, GeSY, organised with the Health Insurance Organisation. Being on the island is not the same as being a beneficiary. The practical map is in the resident handbook: a beneficiary record and a place on a personal doctor’s list are separate steps, and the handbook’s account of the Organisation’s own pages is that a non-Cypriot needs a migration record and a valid residence permit before applying. The relocation guide puts that step among the other clocks, next to the residence file, the tax code and a payment account. A yellow slip or a pink slip is not itself a GeSY card.

That leaves a gap. A visitor is not a beneficiary. A person who has just arrived may be weeks away from a completed file. Care outside Cyprus is not what GeSY is for. A private clinic may ask for payment or a guarantee before it treats you. This page does not list which clinic bills which insurer. For the months before a public system will take you, or for travel away from Cyprus, a private policy is the document that is actually in force. Contribution rates are on gesy.org.cy and they change, so they are not repeated here.

Emergency services in the European Union, Cyprus included, are reached on 112. A policy does not replace that call.

Genki, the cover this guide points to

For international health insurance aimed at people outside their home country, this guide points to Genki. Genki sells cover for travellers and for people who stay abroad longer, not a single packaged holiday. It publishes plans shaped for a trip and plans shaped for living away. The current product names, the price, the excess, the maximum and the exclusions are on Genki’s own site. They are not copied here. A copied price is how a review goes stale the week the insurer changes it.

Read the schedule Genki shows you, not only the homepage. Mark whether the plan is for a trip or for residence, and read the insurer named on the certificate, which may not be the brand on the website. Mark the excess and the maximum, any lower cap on evacuation or dental care, and whether a home country is assumed. Mark conditions you already have, care in the United States, care in the country where you already live, waiting periods, and maternity or mental-health care if those matter to you. Mark whether you can start the plan after you have already moved, from which day a claim is possible, and whether you must call an assistance line before treatment that is not an emergency.

A commission may be paid if you buy through the button. That does not change the wording of the policy, and it does not mean the plan is suitable. If the button is still a placeholder, the real address has not been added. This is not a star rating and not a claim that every other insurer is a worse fit. Another contract may take a condition this one postpones, or suit a short trip better than a family that has settled. The honest method is to put Genki’s schedule next to the questions above and mark the clauses you actually need.

A phone that still rings

An insurer’s assistance line and a clinic both need a way to reach you. So does a bank that sends a one-time code. A travel eSIM is a way to get mobile data, and sometimes a number, without hunting for a shop on the day you land. Airalo sells travel eSIM plans for many countries. The countries, the data amounts and the prices are on Airalo’s site and they change. This page does not quote them.

Many travel eSIMs are data plans. Do not assume a data eSIM will receive the text message a bank or an insurer sends to the number you had at home. Check whether the plan includes a phone number at all, and give the insurer and your bank a number that will actually receive the message. An eSIM is not a health policy. It does not pay a hospital. It keeps the phone online so the policy you did buy can be used.

Roaming on the SIM you already have, or a local Cyprus SIM once a shop will sell you one, can do the same job. The point is reachability, not a particular brand.

How to read any policy before you pay

Take the schedule, not the homepage. Mark the insurer’s legal name, the excess and the maximum, any lower cap on evacuation or dental care, the start date and waiting period, and whether an illness that began before that date is excluded. Mark the countries, including any special rule for the United States and for the country where you live. Mark the number you must call before treatment that is not an emergency, and the cooling-off rule if the certificate does not match the page you bought from.

Keep the certificate and that number somewhere other than only the inbox of an address you might lose when you change phones. The guide on protecting accounts you use from abroad is the practical pair to this one. A policy you cannot log in to is cover you cannot use.

If you are comparing the cost of living with the cost of a policy, do not add them into one number. The relocation guide already refuses to print a single monthly cost for Limassol or Paphos, because no official source it uses publishes one. A premium belongs in its own column, next to rent, and only after you have seen the current figure on the insurer’s page.

What this page will not tell you

It will not tell you that you are covered. Only the certificate does that, and only for the facts you declared. It will not tell you that GeSY will accept you, or that a card from another country still works after you have moved your life to Cyprus. It will not estimate a claim, and it will not decide a Genki schedule for you. For a condition, a pregnancy, or a move that changes where you are insured, ask the insurer and the institution that covers you now, and keep the reply.

Questions readers ask

Is travel insurance enough if I live abroad?

A travel medical policy is built for a trip. Living abroad can put you outside its idea of home, and it usually does not cover routine care. If you have settled, read whether the contract still treats you as a traveller.

Does GeSY replace an international policy?

GeSY is Cyprus’s public system for people who are enrolled. A visitor, or a new arrival who is not yet a beneficiary, is not covered merely by being in the country. It also does not insure care while you travel outside Cyprus. The enrolment steps are in the resident handbook.

Does the European Health Insurance Card cover a move to Cyprus?

The European Commission says the card is for a temporary stay, and that someone who moves and makes the new country their habitual residence should register with an S1 instead of relying on the card. It is not private insurance and it does not pay for a flight home.

Can I buy cover after I have already left home?

Some plans allow it and some do not. A waiting period, or an exclusion for anything that started before the start date, is common. Check that clause on the plan in front of you. This page does not know which rule your quote uses.

Will a plan cover a condition I already have?

Sometimes, sometimes only an acute flare-up, sometimes not at all, sometimes for an extra price. The schedule is the only reliable answer. Do not infer it from a marketing page.

Does Genki replace a doctor in Cyprus?

No. Genki sells insurance. It is not a clinic and it is not GeSY. Whether a particular doctor will bill Genki directly is a question for the insurer and the clinic.
