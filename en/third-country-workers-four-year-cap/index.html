Cyprus drops the extra approval some non-EU workers needed after four years

Reports on Friday said a ministerial committee removed the special approval that general-employment workers from outside the EU needed to renew a residence and work permit after four years. The jobs named include kitchen staff, carers, waiters, bakeries and cheese-makers.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 21 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 21 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

On Friday Philenews and the Cyprus Mail reported that a ministerial committee had abolished an extra approval for non-EU citizens working in the general employment sector. Until now, once four years were up, renewing a residence and work permit required a special approval. That additional requirement, the same reports said, has been removed. Both accounts rest on the Cyprus Federation of Private Employment Agencies, which belongs to the Cyprus Chamber of Commerce and Industry, Keve.

The reports name occupations in general employment: kitchen assistants, carers, waiters, and workers in bakeries and cheese-making. They describe the change for that sector. They do not say the same abolition covers every other kind of permit.

The federation says the change should make it easier for businesses to keep people who are already trained, and to spend less on constantly replacing staff, on paperwork and on delays. Federation president Iacovos Pembedjian said the federation had long asked for this and that the decision matches the real labour market. “This is an important step towards employment stability, the retention of expertise and the strengthening of productivity and competitiveness among Cypriot businesses,” he said, as the Cyprus Mail reported.

The federation also says digital reforms in the services that handle the hiring of foreign staff have to be carried out so that the procedures actually work. Otherwise, it says, life will not get easier for agency staff and for businesses. It says it will keep proposing ways for the state to simplify the hiring of workers from third countries.

The Cyprus Mail also describes an expanded federation meeting on 12 November, with more than 120 people from private agencies across the island. They discussed a digital platform for applications, understaffing in the departments, and the need for applications to be filed only by authorised and licensed professionals, so as to prevent exploitation and unfair competition. The paper says the federation acknowledges the load on current public staff and still considers them too few.
