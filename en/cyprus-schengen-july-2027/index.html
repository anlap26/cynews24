Cyprus to join the Schengen zone on 1 July 2027, Mitsotakis says

Cyprus Schengen 2027: Mitsotakis says the island joins on 1 July, the day Greece takes the EU Council presidency. The Green Line question is still open.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 18 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 18 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis said Cyprus will join the Schengen zone officially on 1 July 2027, the Cyprus Mail reported on Wednesday. He spoke at the end of the Greece–Cyprus intergovernmental summit. The date is the day Greece takes over the rotating presidency of the Council of the European Union. “We are looking forward to Cyprus joining the Schengen zone officially on July 1, with this potentially being the first significant decision taken under the Greek presidency,” he said. The paper said the line echoed what President Nikos Christodoulides had told it the week before.

Christodoulides had said that “the objections expressed by member states” to Cyprus joining “have been largely overcome”. He has not said whether, or how, the zone’s digital entry/exit system for third-country nationals would be used at the checkpoints between the two sides of the island. The Cyprus Mail says those checkpoints will soon number 11.

The gap the paper describes is about what gets recorded. Someone could land at Ercan (Tymbou) and then cross the Green Line, or arrive from a Schengen country and leave through Ercan. Either way, the entry or the exit would not be entered in the system. If that stayed as it is, the Mail writes, one Schengen state would be unable to record part of the arrivals and departures of third-country nationals, and the administration of Europe’s borders would break down.

Christodoulides told the paper last week that Turkish Cypriots and members of their families who already have the right to use the crossings “will not be affected”, and that they would keep the same rights they have now to move between the two sides.

Justice and home affairs ministers left Cyprus and Schengen off the agenda when they last met, at the start of October. The next meetings are on 3 and 4 December. The Mail says that session may dwell more on a proposed return hub for irregular migrants in Rwanda, a scheme Cyprus has said it wants to join.

In July the European Commission set out its support for Cyprus joining. A diplomat, speaking to the paper without a name, said Ursula von der Leyen had pressed member states to accept it, after years in which the European view was that Cyprus could not join while the Cyprus problem was unsolved.

Turkish Cypriot leader Tufan Erhurman has said it is “extremely important” that the Turkish Cypriot side is involved, because joining could bring complications for freedom of movement, above all for Turkish Cypriots who are not citizens of the Republic. Cyprus and Ireland are the only EU countries still outside Schengen. Ireland stays out so as not to unsettle its common travel area with the United Kingdom.

A Schengen stamp is not a health policy. The breakdown of international health insurance separates a travel medical plan, a longer stay and GeSY. Wednesday’s summit opened on a different agenda, including artificial intelligence and civil protection.
