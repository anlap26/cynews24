Microsoft says the global outage is over and traces it to a July software defect

The company said on Saturday that service was restored. A preliminary report links the failure to a July change in the network gateways that surfaced on 12 November.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 13 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 16 November 2026, 16:00 (EET)

Microsoft said on Saturday, 14 November, that it had finished work on the underlying problem and restored service to the remaining customers and regions hit by the outage. The company posted that on X. Associated Press and Deutsche Welle reported the statement. The disruption, which affected Outlook, Teams and Copilot, lasted more than 24 hours.

On Friday, 13 November, the failure was still under way. It reached Asia-Pacific business hours after first hitting thousands of users in the United States. Microsoft was then blaming server problems. Government agencies in Australia, Japan and the United States were affected, as were companies including OpenAI and Anthropic. Email through Outlook and Exchange Online was disrupted in several regions, and some Teams and Copilot users were still reporting trouble. Adjustments applied to the affected servers, the company said, had not produced a lasting recovery.

A preliminary post-incident report, published by Microsoft on 15 November and set out by The Register on Monday, traces the defect to July 2026: it was introduced in a change prepared for Microsoft 365 network gateway servers. Microsoft says the update passed load testing at the time and was deployed without impact.

On 12 November a Teams failover sent traffic to the primary gateway that supports Exchange Online, at the same time as almost 14 per cent of that gateway's server capacity was out of service. The Register, citing the report, says neither event was unusual on its own. Together they pushed the gateway into the defect. Within two hours it had spread across the fleet. At the lowest point, more than 80 per cent of servers were unresponsive.

The report says the usual automated recovery was not designed for a failure that wide, and a straightforward rollback was not possible because the defect was months old. Engineers applied an update that returned the code to the previously known good state, in groups of servers. On the report's timeline, the mitigation was identified and applied at 07:20 UTC on 13 November. Microsoft judged the service healthy again at 16:10 UTC the same day, and the final customer update went out at 04:15 UTC on 14 November. A final report is due on 20 November.

Microsoft says it is not aware of outside interference. Updates that the Cyprus Mail reported while the outage was still going on also said there was no indication of a cyberattack.
