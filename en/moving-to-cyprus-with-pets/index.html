Moving to Cyprus with a dog or a cat: the papers and the lease

Dogs, cats and ferrets need a microchip, a rabies vaccination at least 21 days old, and a passport or a health certificate. The lease is the other half of the move.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 27 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 27 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

A dog or a cat can come to Cyprus. The animal needs a microchip, a valid rabies vaccination, and either an EU pet passport or an official health certificate, depending on the country it travels from. The government service page is transport of animals. The detailed conditions for passengers are on the Customs page about restrictions on importation, which names the Veterinary Services as the authority for pet dogs, cats and ferrets. As of 2026, check both pages. Airline rules and the Veterinary Services notices move, and a blog’s checklist from a previous move is how animals get held at the airport.

What the animal needs

The non-commercial rules, as Customs states them for dogs, cats and ferrets, come down to a short list.

  • An electronic microchip, readable. A tattoo is accepted only if it was applied before 3 July 2011 and is still clearly readable. Chip the animal before the rabies vaccination, or the vaccination may not count.
  • A rabies vaccination, given when the animal was at least 12 weeks old, with an inactivated vaccine to the standard the notice describes. The vaccination has to have been given at least 21 days before the animal arrives. A jab the week before the flight does not qualify.
  • A health certificate, or a passport from a country that uses the EU model. Inside the EU, the pet passport issued by an authorised vet is the document owners already have. From outside the EU, expect a veterinary health certificate issued close to the date of travel, and check whether a rabies antibody titre from an EU-approved laboratory is required for the country you are leaving. The titre, where it is required, has to be done long enough before travel that the waiting period has finished. That wait is measured in months, not in days. Start it before you book the flight.
  • No more than five pet animals travelling with the owner or an authorised person on one non-commercial movement. Six animals is no longer a family move under that rule. It starts to look like trade, which is a different licence.

Ferrets are in the same pet rules. Rabbits, birds and other species are not. Turtles have their own forms and a quarantine step on the same Customs page. Do not assume the dog rules cover anything else.

Chip before the vaccine

A rabies vaccination given before the microchip can be treated as if it had not happened. Do the chip first.

The photograph is a pet cat. It is not an animal in a Cyprus airport queue. The rules depend on the country of dispatch and on the species, not on the colour of the coat.

Five animals, not six

Six pets on one movement start to look like trade. The passenger rule stops at five.

The flight

The airline is a second set of rules on top of the state rules. Cabin or hold, the crate size, the breed restrictions some carriers apply to short-nosed dogs, the temperature embargo in summer, and the fee are all in the carrier’s pet policy. A state-compliant animal can still be refused by the airline. Book the animal as a pet, in the way that carrier requires, rather than arriving at check-in with a carrier bag and a hope.

In summer, the hold of an aircraft on the apron in Cyprus or in a Gulf hub can be too hot for a brachycephalic dog even when the paperwork is perfect. Ask the airline what they do in July. If the answer is vague, choose a different date or a different route. Direct flights reduce the number of times the crate is moved.

On arrival, use the channel Customs expects for a declaration when the notice says to declare. Do not walk the green channel with an animal because the queue is shorter. The Veterinary Services at the airport are the people who will look at the passport or the certificate. Have the microchip number, the vaccination page and the certificate in a folder you can open with one hand.

After you land

An EU pet passport from another member state continues to be the animal’s document. Keep the rabies vaccination in date. A lapse means starting the 21-day wait again before the animal can travel onward, which matters the first time you want to leave the island and come back.

Register the animal where a municipality requires it. Dogs in particular are often subject to a local licence and to rules about leashes and fouling that are enforced unevenly and still exist. Ask the municipality of the town you will live in, not a national stereotype. Vets in all four cities can update a passport and will know the local expectation. The first appointment is worth booking before you need a signature in a hurry.

There is no substitute, on this page, for the Veterinary Services if the country you are leaving is an unusual one or the animal is a rescue with a thin history. Write to them with the microchip number, the vaccination dates and the country before you buy the ticket. The address and the notices are reached from the Veterinary Services site. As of 2026, use the contact on that site rather than a phone number copied from a forum.

Housing

The hard part, for many people, is not the certificate. It is the lease. A large share of Cyprus leases forbid pets, or allow a cat and forbid a dog, or allow a dog under a weight the landlord invented. Negotiate before you sign. An animal that arrives into a building whose rules forbid it will be the tenant’s problem, and the residence file that depends on that address becomes fragile if you have to move again in a month.

When you view a flat, ask to see the house rules of the building as well as the lease. A landlord who says yes and a management committee that says no is a common outcome in a new block. Get the permission in the lease, naming the animal or the species and the weight if they insist on a number. An oral “we love dogs” does not survive a change of building manager.

Rentals that accept pets are easier to find in houses and in older low-rise blocks than in a new tower with a marble lobby. The search takes longer. Budget for that in the rent ranges, and look at which city actually has the stock. A village house can be ideal for a dog and awkward for every other reason, including the summer heat in a car and the distance to a vet who is open on a Sunday.

Other animals and the edge cases

A puppy too young to be vaccinated against rabies cannot travel under the standard pet rules. Waiting until the animal is old enough, vaccinated, and past the 21 days is the lawful sequence. Sedating an animal for the flight is something to decide with a vet, not with a forum: many carriers forbid sedation because of the risk in the hold.

Commercial imports, adoption schemes that are in substance a trade, and animals that are not dogs, cats or ferrets need the licence the Veterinary Services issue for that movement. The passenger rules on the Customs page are the wrong page for a container of animals. If you are relocating a household that includes a horse or a bird, start with the Veterinary Services months ahead.

The person’s own residence route is separate. The animal’s papers do not help a visa, and a visa does not waive the microchip. The residence routes are in moving to Cyprus.

Useful services

  • An authorised vet in the country of departure, for the chip, the vaccine, the titre if it is required, and the certificate or passport. Book the vet before the flights.
  • An airline that will carry the species in the season you are flying. Read the embargo dates.
  • A vet in Cyprus for the week after arrival, so a stomach upset on new food is not an emergency search.
  • A landlord who has put the pet in the lease. The property consultation can help you look for a rental that allows one. It cannot change a building’s rules.

Property search and a document check

If you are looking for a rental or a house where a dog or a cat is written into the contract, use the property consultation. Say that you have a pet, name the species, and name the cities you will consider. The form asks for a name and an email. It is not a check of the animal’s papers. Questions readers ask

Can I bring a dog or a cat?

Yes, as a non-commercial movement, with a microchip, a valid rabies vaccination given at least 21 days before arrival, and an EU pet passport or an official health certificate. The maximum is five animals.

Does a puppy qualify?

Not until it is old enough to be vaccinated and the 21 days have passed. From some countries a rabies antibody test adds a wait measured in months. Start before you book.

Will the airline accept the animal if the state does?

Not necessarily. Cabin or hold, the crate, the breed and the summer heat rules are the carrier’s. A lawful animal can still be refused at check-in.

Are pets allowed in rentals?

Often no. Get the permission into the lease, and check the building’s rules. An oral yes does not survive a change of manager.

Who is the authority?

The Veterinary Services, reached through gov.cy and the ministry’s veterinary site. Customs publishes the passenger conditions. As of 2026, use those pages.
