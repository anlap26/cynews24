Fitto is due to meet Fidan in Ankara today, while Kokkina stays on the table

The Commission’s envoy for Cyprus is in the Turkish capital for talks on the Cyprus problem and EU-Turkey relations. Christodoulides told Tylliria communities, Politis reported, that a Kokkina crossing is still under discussion.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 20 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 20 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

European Commission executive vice-president and special envoy for Cyprus Raffaele Fitto travels to Ankara on Friday, 20 November, to meet Turkish foreign minister Hakan Fidan. The Cyprus problem and the EU’s relations with Turkey are the subject, Kathimerini reported, citing the Cyprus News Agency, and so did Politis. Morning reports described the meeting as set for today. None of them carried an outcome.

Kathimerini called the conversation particularly important ahead of a trilateral meeting that the UN secretary-general is expected to call soon with Christodoulides and Turkish Cypriot leader Tufan Erhurman. On the sidelines of the recent climate conference in Antalya, the president discussed the Cyprus talks, EU-Turkey relations and Nicosia’s Schengen accession with Commission president Ursula von der Leyen. The agency said they also discussed preparations for Fitto’s visit to Turkey, and that there was contact with Fitto himself.

Last week Christodoulides said Nicosia expects positive developments in EU-Turkey relations “which can only materialise if there are developments on the Cyprus problem, and not more general developments”. He also spoke of restarting talks on the agreed basis, from the point where they stopped at Crans-Montana. Government spokesman Konstantinos Letymbiotis said on Wednesday, Politis reported, that if the peace talks resume from Crans-Montana, Nicosia would without doubt consent to opening discussions on modernising Turkey’s customs union with the EU. Politis described that upgrade as economically attractive for Ankara: Turkish firms could gain wider access to EU services and public-procurement markets, with reciprocal access for European companies to the Turkish market. The paper left open what Fitto will actually put on the table, and how Ankara will answer.

The two leaders’ next meeting is set for Tuesday, 24 November, at 3pm. The details of that arrangement are in a separate article. Letymbiotis repeated on Thursday that discussions on substance and methodology are continuing ahead of the joint meeting under the UN secretary-general, with the steady aim of substantial progress towards restarting negotiations from where they stopped in 2017. Erhurman has recently stressed, Politis wrote, that there can be no progress without political equality and a rotating presidency, and that both he and Ankara see the Greek Cypriot side’s unwillingness to share power and resources as the basic obstacle.

The Turkish agency ANKA, Politis reported, said Fidan had the day before repeated Ankara’s position to a parliamentary committee in Turkey: “two separate peoples, two separate republics and two separate states” in Cyprus. He added that Turkey seeks a just and lasting solution based on the “realities of the island”. Some observers, the paper wrote, believe Fidan’s visit to the occupied areas a few days before the leaders’ trilateral with the secretary-general’s personal envoy, María Ángela Holguín, stopped the announcement of four new crossing points. Politis set out the questions the Ankara meeting could move: whether Ankara will approve the use of a stretch of road in the buffer zone to link Athienou with Aglantzia, whether it will change its language on the basis of a settlement, and whether it will back Erhurman in starting talks from Crans-Montana.

Politis also reported on Kokkina. The paper said Christodoulides told representatives of the Tylliria communities on Thursday that opening a crossing at the Turkish military enclave of Kokkina remains on the table. In a recent interview with the same paper, Holguín had said categorically that Kokkina was never put on the table, because the Turkish Cypriot side had made clear from the start that it was not an option, and Erhurman later repeated that. Sources confirmed to Politis that the Turkish army had ruled out any move on Kokkina during one of the enlarged meetings in 2025, despite an alternative proposal from the British delegation.

After his meeting with the president, the head of the Tylliria communities, Nikos Kleanthous, said: “We emphasised the opening of the Kokkina crossing. The president assured us the issue is on the table of the discussions, and we expect positive results and developments regarding our crossing as well.” He said an opening would ease movement to and from the area and cut travel time by about 40 minutes. Told that it is an entirely military area, Kleanthous replied that, in their view, the whole of the north is militarised, with camps scattered as far as Morphou. “We must not forget that the militarily controlled area of Kokkina is supplied every day through our own community,” he said, adding that there are ways to make an opening feasible.
