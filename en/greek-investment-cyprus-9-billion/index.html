Greek direct investment in Cyprus rose to €9.2 billion

Greek investment in Cyprus reached €9.2 billion by June 2026, from €5.6 billion in 2024, Invest Cyprus said. Cypriot investment in Greece is about €2.7 billion.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 18 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 18 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

The stock of Greek direct investment in Cyprus reached €9.2 billion in June 2026, up from €5.6 billion in 2024, Invest Cyprus chairman Evgenios Evgeniou said on Wednesday. He was speaking at the 4th Greece–Cyprus Strategic Horizons Forum in Nicosia. Phileleftheros and the Cyprus Business News service both reported the figures. Cypriot investment in Greece is estimated at about €2.7 billion, and more than 1,500 companies with Greek interests operate in Cyprus. Phileleftheros used that wording; the business service described them as Greek-owned or Greek-affiliated. Trade in goods between the two countries reached €3.3 billion in 2025, and exports of goods produced in Cyprus to Greece rose by 12 per cent, both reports said.

Evgeniou said cooperation with Greece is a priority for Invest Cyprus. The memorandum with Enterprise Greece was renewed in April, which he said opened the way for new investment initiatives, business missions and an exchange of expertise. At a recent meeting with Greece’s deputy foreign minister and the head of Enterprise Greece, he said, it was agreed to turn the memorandum into a joint action plan with specific initiatives and measurable results.

In early December, he announced, Invest Cyprus will hold its first closed working meeting with Greek businesses that operate in Cyprus, together with the Greek embassy. Cyprus’s minister of energy, commerce and industry and the head of Enterprise Greece are due to attend, to identify what the companies need and where new investment might lie. He singled out the defence industry, defence technology and energy as fields where the two countries’ strengths complement each other and could help draw international capital. He pointed to the Great Sea Interconnector, the electricity link, as an example of the coordination large cross-border projects require. The state of that project is in this report.

Nicosia mayor Charalambos Prountzos, also at the forum, said the city aims to lift tourism revenue from about €170 million to €400 million by 2029, Phileleftheros reported. He said the municipality wants Nicosia’s share of Cyprus’s tourist arrivals to rise from 5.7 per cent to 10 per cent. The number of tourist beds in the capital is expected to double by 2027 through hotel investments worth more than €250 million, he said, and major investments are also under way in the historic centre. He described a planned regeneration of the Sopaz area in east Nicosia, covering 1.4 million square metres, as “a new city within the city”, promoted with the government and meant to join the area’s industrial past to business, innovation and daily life. He invited Greek firms to look at the capital.

Phileleftheros also quoted Vicky Makrygianni of the Hellenic Federation of Enterprises and Andreas Demetriades of the Cyprus Chamber of Commerce. Both described closer cooperation, including in third markets, as a goal that still needs an action plan.
