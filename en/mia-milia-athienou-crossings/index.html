Letymbiotis: Cyprus is "very close" on the Mia Milia and Athienou–Pyroi crossings

Nicosia is waiting for the UN to date the next trilateral, and says the president has not accepted Erhürman's proposal. The €25 million for the electricity link has not been paid.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 16 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 16 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Government spokesman Konstantinos Letymbiotis said on Monday 16 November, on ANT1, that on two further crossing points — Mia Milia and Athienou–Pyroi — the sides "were and remain very close. We are there, to be precise". These are points beyond the two agreed on 9 November, when Nikos Christodoulides, Tufan Erhürman and the UN secretary-general's personal envoy María Ángela Holguín met. On Athienou–Pyroi, he said, the secretary-general has already tabled a compromise proposal for the route. What remains, he said, is a political decision by the Turkish Cypriot side.

Nicosia is waiting for the secretary-general's office to set the date and place of the next trilateral meeting. The aim is to hold it as soon as possible, so that an enlarged conference can follow and substantive negotiations can resume within António Guterres's term. The meeting, Letymbiotis said, should not be limited to confidence-building measures: "What we are seeking is a discussion on the substance and the methodology, so that we can move to the next step." Contacts between the two leaders and the negotiators will continue, he said, as last week's joint statement provides.

On Kokkina, he said the frustration of residents of Pyrgos Tillirias was understandable. The point was among the Greek Cypriot side's first proposals and was discussed at the enlarged meetings in Geneva and New York, but it runs into "the categorical and absolute refusal of the occupation army". Christodoulides, the spokesman said, has submitted two alternative proposals for the area; the latest is part of a package of 19. Letymbiotis condemned threats against the deputy mayor in Lympia and said the argument over crossings should not become a confrontation between citizens.

Erhürman's proposal, which the Turkish Cypriot leader had presented as something that could change the picture, has not been accepted by the president. Every idea, Letymbiotis said, is judged in Nicosia on three tests: whether it fits international law and UN Security Council resolutions, whether it helps restart substantive talks, and whether it can improve daily life. He described Ankara's insistence on two states as disregard for that law. The Republic's answer, he said, will be consistency and continued initiatives to restart negotiations.

On the visit to Ankara by European Commission executive vice-president Raffaele Fitto, Letymbiotis said Nicosia wants to see how Turkey responds to linking progress in EU–Turkey relations with progress on the Cyprus problem. "We want to give Turkey incentives and not to isolate it," he said, repeating that European progress has to move at the same time as, in proportion to, and reversibly with progress on Cyprus.

In the same interview he confirmed that the €25 million the Republic has committed to the Greece–Cyprus electricity link has not yet been paid. The procedures, he said, have already started under the regulatory decisions and the agreed roadmap, and he gave no date: "It is a matter of procedures and time that have to be followed." Two navigational warnings were issued for the related surveys, he said, one by Greece and one by Cyprus, and there was no other arrangement that would change the licensing position. "The two states which have sovereign rights are Greece and Cyprus." He would not assess the Aristotle University study: he had seen the press reports but could not speak for the competent ministry. He tied the value of the link above all to energy security.
