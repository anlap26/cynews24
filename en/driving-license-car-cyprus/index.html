Driving in Cyprus: licences, insurance, the MOT and registering a car

Traffic keeps left. A foreign licence can be used for a limited time and, if the country is recognised, exchanged after 185 days of normal residence.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 27 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 27 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Cyprus drives on the left. The steering wheel is on the right. A driver who has only ever driven on the right needs a quiet hour, not a brave one, the first time they leave the airport road. This page is the licence, the insurance, the roadworthiness test and the registration of a car you own. Hiring a car for a week is a different market and is covered in the car hire guide. The rules below are the Road Transport Department’s, and they are the ones to check as of 2026 if a detail here has moved. The department’s ordinary-licence page is on gov.cy. The conversion instructions are on the department’s foreign-licence page.

Left-hand traffic

The sign at the kerb is not a joke and not a souvenir. You keep left, you overtake on the right, and the roundabouts run the other way from the ones in continental Europe. Speed limits are posted in kilometres per hour. The main motorways that link Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca and Paphos are good. The village road with a stone wall and an oncoming bus is the one that hurts. Drink-driving limits are low by the standards of some countries people arrive from. A taxi home is cheaper than the file.

Seatbelts, a child seat that matches the child’s weight, and a phone that stays out of your hand are enforced, including by cameras. The fine is not a surprise if you read the road-safety notices. It is a surprise if you drive as you did in a place where the camera programme was a rumour.

The first hour

Leave yourself a quiet road, not the motorway, the first time you pull out. Roundabouts run the other way from continental Europe.

Using a foreign licence

You may drive on a valid foreign licence for a limited period after you become resident. The department’s conversion page draws a line that matters. Licences issued by an EU member state, and licences from the other countries the Commissioner has recognised as equivalent, can be exchanged. Holders of an EU licence may exchange after six months of stay if they wish. Holders of a licence from a recognised non-EU country are required to exchange after six months of stay. A licence from a country that is not on that list is not an exchange. It is a Cypriot test: theory and practical, after a learner permit. Do not discover which list you are on in the seventh month.

The page states the residence test used for the exchange: normal residence of at least 185 days. A non-EU citizen is also asked for the alien-registration certificate and the residence permit. A student shows attendance at a higher-education institution for at least six months. The 185 days are not the 183 days of the tax test, and they are not the four months of the MEU1 deadline. Three clocks, three offices. The residence routes are in moving to Cyprus.

Three different clocks

185 days for the licence exchange, 183 days for tax residence, and four months for MEU1 are not the same test and not the same office.

Exchanging the licence

The conversion pack, as the department lists it, is form TOM 7D, a recent photograph, identity or the alien-registration certificate, the passport, the foreign licence and a copy of every page, and a translation if the licence is not in Greek or English. The translation goes through the Press and Information Office’s authorised translators. You attend in person at the district office. The foreign licence is sent to the authority that issued it, so you should not plan to use it again the next week. A driver aged 70 or over, and a driver exchanging a heavy-vehicle category, needs a medical certificate. The ordinary licence fee the department’s other page quotes is €40, for the issue of an ordinary licence when the papers are complete, in about half an hour. Confirm the fee for a conversion on the day, because a conversion is not the same counter as a first licence. As of 2026, the page is the fee.

You cannot hold a second valid EU licence alongside the Cypriot one. The exchange replaces it. If you need the original back for a reason, ask before you hand it over, not after it has been posted to the other authority.

Insurance

Motor insurance is compulsory. A car on the road without it is not a paperwork problem for later. It is an offence, and it is an uninsured loss if anything happens. The policy residents buy is third-party at the minimum, and comprehensive if the car is worth insuring. The premium depends on your age, the car, the claims history and whether you can produce a no-claims letter from the insurer in the country you left. Get that letter before you leave. Reconstructing it from abroad is slow, and the Cypriot insurer will rate you as a new driver until it arrives.

The policy has to match the use. A private car used for a job that is really hire, or a courtesy car driven by someone not named, is the argument you do not want after a crash. Read the certificate. Carry it. The police ask.

A hire car’s insurance is the hire company’s product, with an excess. That is not the policy you will have on a car you buy. Do not compare the two premiums as if they were the same risk.

The MOT, which is the roadworthiness test

A car of a certain age has to pass a periodic technical inspection. People call it the MOT because that is the British name for the same idea. The Cypriot test is the one at the authorised inspection centres, against the Cypriot standard, at the interval the law sets for the age and the type of the vehicle. A failed test is a list of repairs and a retest, not a negotiation. A car that will not pass is a car to walk away from, however attractive the price. Ask for the last certificate before you buy, and look at the date.

Budget a couple of hours and the inspection fee, which the centre posts. This page does not print the fee because the centres revise it. As of 2026, ask the centre.

Registering a car, and bringing one in

A car already on Cypriot plates is a change of ownership at the Road Transport Department: the logbook, the seller’s identity, yours, a valid inspection if one is due, insurance in your name, and the fee. Do not pay the seller in full against a promise to “do the papers next week”. The papers are the purchase.

A car you bring from abroad is a different and heavier file. It needs to be registered, it may need to be modified to left-hand-traffic specification if it is a left-hand-drive car, and it can attract import taxes and VAT depending on where it comes from and on your residence status when it arrives. EU rules on transferring a vehicle when you transfer your residence exist and have conditions, including time limits. A car that arrives six months after you do, or a car you have owned for a fortnight, may not be the car those rules imagine. Get the Customs answer, in writing, before the vehicle is shipped. The shipping cost is lost if the duty makes the car pointless. Customs is the authority. A Facebook group is not.

Right-hand-drive cars are the natural fit. A left-hand-drive car can be registered in some cases and is tiring on a motorway, because overtaking becomes a guess. For a car you will keep, buy local.

Fines, parking and the practical week

Pay a fine when it arrives. The window is shorter than people from some countries expect, and an unpaid notice becomes a larger notice. Parking in the centres of Nicosia and Limassol is a paid habit, not a hope. A resident’s bay, where a municipality offers one, is worth applying for if you live in the old town. The bus is a real option along some corridors and a poor option for a weekly shop in Paphos. The cost of owning the car belongs in the monthly budget.

A driving licence is also one of the documents banks and employers recognise as ordinary life. It is not a residence permit. Do not start the exchange before the 185 days are real. The department checks.

Useful services

  • The district office of the Road Transport Department, for the exchange and the registration, with the originals.
  • An insurer who will put the no-claims letter against a quote before you buy the car, so the premium is a known number.
  • An inspection centre, before you buy a used car rather than after.
  • The car hire guide for the weeks before the 185 days are up, or for a visitor who will not exchange anything.

Property search and a document check

Where you live decides whether the car is essential. A flat in central Nicosia and a house outside Paphos are not the same transport problem, and not the same insurance postcode. Questions readers ask

Which side of the road is it?

The left. The wheel is on the right. Roundabouts run the other way from continental Europe. Speed limits are in kilometres per hour.

When do I have to exchange my licence?

The department says a non-EU licence from a recognised country must be exchanged after six months, and that the residence test is at least 185 days. An EU licence may be exchanged after six months. An unrecognised licence means taking the Cypriot tests.

Is insurance compulsory?

Yes. Third party at a minimum. Get a no-claims letter from your old insurer before you leave, or you will be priced as a new driver.

What is the MOT?

The periodic roadworthiness test at an authorised centre, for a car old enough to need one. See the last certificate before you buy a used car.

Can I bring my car from abroad?

Sometimes, and the duty depends on where the car comes from and on your residence timing. Ask Customs before you ship it. A left-hand-drive car is a poor long-term fit.
