Renting a car in Cyprus: prices, insurance and driving rules

Car hire in Cyprus: drive on the left, 100 km/h maximum and 65 minimum on major highways, 50 in town unless a sign says otherwise. There is no single official daily hire price.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 19 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 19 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

The state does not publish one daily price for a hire car. The firm sets it, and it moves with the season, the excess and what the contract includes. Below are the rules the government itself prints, and what one large hire company writes about insurance. This is not a tariff, and it is not a claim that every firm works the same way.

Which side, and how fast

The road-safety leaflet on gov.cy says it plainly: in Cyprus you drive on the left, not the right. On the major highways the maximum is 100 km/h and the minimum is 65 km/h. On ordinary inter-urban and rural roads, unless a sign says otherwise, the maximum is 80 km/h. In built-up areas the maximum is 50 km/h, unless a sign sets another limit. A lower posted limit beats the general number.

On a highway you keep the left lane. The right lane is for overtaking, for when the left lane is not free, and to let a vehicle join. Stopping and parking on a highway are forbidden. So are pedestrians, bicycles, mopeds and agricultural machines.

Seatbelts are obligatory in the front and the back. A child under 1.5 metres uses a suitable restraint. A hand-held phone is prohibited; hands-free only. Alcohol: not above 50 milligrams per 100 millilitres of blood, and not above 22 micrograms per 100 millilitres of breath.

The leaflet tells pedestrians that traffic is on the left, so they look right first. Where there is no pavement, they walk on the right, facing the cars.

Licences

The same leaflet splits drivers into groups. European citizens drive on their own country’s licence. A visitor from outside Europe drives on a home licence for at most 30 days, or on a valid international permit. A named list of countries, among them Russia, the United States, Ukraine, Canada, Australia and others, may use a national licence for up to six months and then apply for a Cypriot licence without a test. Anyone outside those groups takes theory and practice. That is the rule for someone who stays and exchanges a licence. A hire firm can ask for age or experience beyond the statute. The government leaflet does not print a minimum hire age, so this page does not invent one.

Insurance, and the north

The leaflet says: be sure you are covered for injury or damage. The Visit Cyprus note on bringing your own car requires a valid insurance certificate with third-party liability, plus registration and a valid licence.

Hertz Cyprus publishes a different text, about its own vehicles: drivers are covered for fire and for third-party liability up to the maxima set by the Republic of Cyprus. The same page says a rental car may not be taken outside the Republic, and may not be driven into the north of the island. If it is, Hertz writes that the insurances cease, and the driver is responsible for the loss and for bringing the car back. That is one firm’s clause, not a statute restated here as a ban on every hire company. Read it in the contract you actually sign before you drive to a crossing.

Two warning triangles, child seats and the excess are not one table in the government leaflet quoted here. Ask the firm. In an emergency, call 112.

Airport transfer

The 2026 tourism booklet names Larnaca (LCA) and Paphos (PFO) as the air gateways. If you would rather not collect a car at the terminal, Welcome Pickups covers transfers at those two airports, and Kiwitaxi is the wider transfer search. Fares are on their pages, not here.

Questions readers ask

Which side of the road is it?

The gov.cy road-safety leaflet says the left, not the right. You overtake on the right.

What speed limits does it name?

Major highways: 100 km/h maximum and 65 minimum. Ordinary inter-urban and rural roads: 80 unless posted otherwise. Built-up areas: 50 unless a sign says otherwise.

Is there one official hire price?

No. The firm publishes the daily rate. The government leaflet does not set a hire price.
