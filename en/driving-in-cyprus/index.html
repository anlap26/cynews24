Driving in Cyprus: left-hand traffic, and whose licence lasts

Traffic keeps left. A visitor leaflet and the Road Transport Department do not describe the same person. EU licences can be kept. Some other licences must be exchanged after six months. Everyone else sits a test.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Cyprus drives on the left. The government's visitor leaflet states it as the fact to read before anything else, because many arrivals come from countries that drive on the right. The rest of the rules depend on whether you are visiting or resident, and on which country issued the licence. Two public texts are used here. They are not duplicates. The leaflet is for a visitor. The Road Transport Department's conversion page is for a person who will exchange a licence.

Visitors

The leaflet says European citizens may drive using their country's licence. It says non-European visitors may drive using their country's licence for a maximum of 30 days, or on a valid international driving permit. It also tells the reader to hold valid insurance for injuries and damage. This page does not add a day to the 30, and it does not interpret an international permit beyond the sentence in the leaflet. If your stay is becoming residence, stop using the leaflet as the rule and read the Department.

Hiring a car is the usual way a visitor drives. The contract, the excess and the fact of left-hand traffic are in renting a car in Cyprus. DiscoverCars is one of the affiliate links for a hire search. The button does not state a price.

Residents, and the six-month line

The Department's conversion page says EU licences are recognised, and that an EU holder may exchange after a six-month stay if they wish. It says holders from countries on the Commissioner's published notice are obliged to exchange after a six-month stay. The exchange of a recognised licence, on that page, does not by itself mean sitting the Cyprus test. A licence from a country that is not an EU state and not on the notice cannot be exchanged that way. The holder applies for a learner licence and takes the test. The Department points to the Driving Licence Law of 2001 and its amendments.

The notice of recognised countries is a legal instrument and it has been replaced before. This guide does not paste a country list from a secondary article. Before you surrender a licence, ask the district office whether the country is recognised now. Giving up the only card you can drive on, in order to wait, is a practical risk the page should not push you into.

The Department asks exchange applicants to show normal residence. Its note says a foreign applicant must have resided in Cyprus for at least 185 days, and a non-EU citizen must show the registration certificate and the residence permit. That 185-day test is the Department's residence test for the licence. It is not the Tax Department's 183-day or 60-day test. The two counts are explained side by side in the site's relocation guide.

Papers on the conversion page include form TOM 7D, a photograph, identity or alien registration, the passport, the foreign licence and every page of it, and a translation unless the licence is in English or Greek, done through the Press and Information Office's authorised translators. The applicant attends in person. The foreign licence, the page says, is sent to the authority that issued it. Read that line before you assume you will keep the old card in a drawer.

Fines and the road itself

Cameras and fixed penalties exist and change. A news report is a better place for a new fine than this guide. Camera fines and the 180-day window is one such report. It is not a highway code.

Speed, drink, and a roundabout taken as if the traffic were on the right are how visitors have collisions. None of that is fixed by owning the right plastic card. Insurance is not a substitute for the side of the road.

Questions readers ask

Which side of the road?

The left. The government's visitor leaflet says so in capital letters. Portugal, if you are comparing countries, drives on the right. Malta, like Cyprus, drives on the left.

Can I drive on my EU licence?

The Road Transport Department says holders of EU licences may keep driving on them, and may exchange after a six-month stay if they wish. The exchange is described as optional for EU licences.

What about a UK or other non-EU licence?

The Department says holders from countries on the Commissioner's notice must exchange after a six-month stay, without describing that exchange as optional. The notice is a legal list and it changes. Ask the district office whether your country is on it before you give the card up. Countries not recognised lead to a Cyprus test.

What can a visitor do?

The visitor leaflet says European citizens may drive on their country's licence, and that non-European visitors may drive on their country's licence for up to 30 days or on a valid international driving permit. A visitor leaflet is not the residence procedure. If you have become resident, use the Department's page.

Is insurance optional?

The visitor leaflet says to have valid insurance for injury and damage. This page does not quote a premium. A hire car's insurance is the hire contract, covered in the car-rental guide.
