Opening a bank account in Cyprus: what no guide can print for you

A residence card is not an account. Banks apply their own checks under the anti-money-laundering rules. The Central Bank lists licensed institutions. This page does not name one as easier, and it does not list a pack of documents a bank has not published as a national form.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Opening a bank account is the step that sounds administrative and is, in practice, a decision by a private institution. Cyprus has a Central Bank, and that Central Bank licenses and supervises credit institutions. The list of who is allowed to take deposits is on the Central Bank's site. A firm that is not on it is not made safer by a smooth website. This guide stops at that public fact. It does not review Bank of Cyprus, Hellenic Bank or any other name, and it does not claim that one of them is currently welcoming to new arrivals. Welcoming is a policy, and policies change without a statute.

What the bank is deciding

Under the anti-money-laundering framework a bank has to know its customer. In ordinary language that means identity, where you live, what you do, and where the money came from. A yellow slip, a pink slip or a nomad permit is evidence of an immigration status. It is not an instruction to the bank to open a ledger. Tax residence is a third status again. The three are separated in the relocation guide and in tax residency. Handing over a residence card and waiting for an IBAN is how people lose weeks.

There is no national checklist this page can honestly print. Banks ask for a passport, proof of address, and papers that show the source of funds. Which papers, how recent, and whether a foreign utility bill counts, is the bank's written list. If a guide elsewhere prints a universal pack, treat it as that author's last successful application, not as law. Ask the institution, and keep the email.

Refusals happen. A refusal is not always explained in a way you can argue with. Nothing on this page is a route around a refusal, and nothing here is an offer to introduce you to a banker. CyNews 24 does not hold client money and does not refer account openings.

Non-residents and companies

A person who is not yet in Cyprus sometimes wants the account first, because a landlord or an immigration file asks for one. Some banks will talk to a non-resident and some will not. This page will not say which, because that would be a claim about a policy it has not read this week. A company account is a different product from a personal account. The company itself is opening a company. The bank will ask who owns it and who directs it. A corporate tax rate does not answer those questions.

The login, once you have it

The risk after the account exists is the login. A one-time code to a number you no longer control, or a password typed on a network you do not know, is how accounts are taken over. Protecting accounts you use from abroad is the practical pair to this page. A travel eSIM can give you data on the day you land. Check whether it can receive the bank's text. Many data eSIMs cannot.

Questions readers ask

Which bank should I use?

This page does not say. The Central Bank of Cyprus publishes the list of licensed credit institutions. A blog that ranks them by how friendly they are to newcomers is not that list.

Will my residence permit open the account?

No. The permit shows an immigration status. The bank still has to identify you, understand where the money comes from and decide whether to take you as a customer. Some applications are refused. This page cannot tell you which.

What documents are required?

There is no single national form this guide can copy. Banks ask for identity, an address, and evidence of the source of funds, and the pack differs. Ask the bank you are actually applying to, and keep what they send.

Can I open an account before I arrive?

Some institutions talk to non-residents and some do not. This page does not know the current policy of a named bank. Remote onboarding, where a bank offers it, is still that bank's process.

How do I protect the login?

A new SIM and a café network are a bad pair for a bank code. The practical page is the guide on protecting accounts you use from abroad. An eSIM is a way to have data. It is not the bank.
