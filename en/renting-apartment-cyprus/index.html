Renting an apartment in Cyprus: prices, deposits, utilities and the lease

Asking rents in 2026 differ sharply by city. The deposit, the electricity bill and the name on the lease matter as much as the monthly figure.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 27 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 27 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

A long-term rental in Cyprus is a private contract, plus a handful of bills that do not sit inside the rent. The market moves, and it moves differently in Limassol, Nicosia, Larnaca and Paphos. The figures below are asking ranges seen in 2026, not an index and not a promise. Check current listings, and treat any single “average rent” in a headline as a marketing line.

What landlords ask per month

These are unfurnished or lightly furnished apartments offered to a long-term tenant, not a week on a booking site. A seafront unit, a new building with a pool, or a furnished executive flat sits at the top of the range or above it. A village house, or an older block away from the water, sits at the bottom.

What sits at the top of the range

A seafront flat or a new tower is not the middle of the table. Use the table as a filter, then open a listing.

The deposit and the first payment

A deposit of one or two months’ rent is the usual request. Some landlords also ask for the first month in advance, so the cash on the day you get the keys can be two or three months’ rent. Get a receipt that says “deposit”, names the property, and says when it comes back. A deposit is the tenant’s money, held against unpaid rent or damage beyond fair wear. It is not a fee.

Agency commission, where an agent introduced the flat, is often the equivalent of one month’s rent, sometimes split and sometimes charged to the tenant. Ask before you view a second time. There is no official tariff this page can print.

Stamp duty on leases was caught by the 2026 tax reform. The Migration Department’s visitor checklist still says a rental agreement should be stamped “where applicable”. For a lease signed on or after 1 January 2026, confirm with the Tax Department whether any duty is still due before you pay a figure from an old calculator. The MyCyTax stamp-duty calculator shows the scale that calculator uses. It is not a bill.

What the lease should say

A one-year term is common. A shorter term is possible and usually costs more. Read these points before you sign.

  • The rent, the day it is due, and the account it is paid into. Pay the person named on the title or the person the title owner has authorised, not a cousin who “handles the keys”.
  • What is included. Common expenses, a pool, a parking bay and a storeroom are often extra.
  • Who pays building insurance, and who pays minor repairs. A leaking boiler in the first week should not be a surprise argument.
  • Whether the tenant may register the address for a residence file, and whether the landlord will sign the declaration some forms ask for.
  • Pets, if you have one. Many leases forbid them. The negotiation is before you sign, not after the animal lands. See moving with a pet.
  • How the tenancy ends: notice, in writing, and what happens to the deposit if you leave early.
  • An inventory with photographs if the flat is furnished. Do it on the day you move in.

A lease can be in Greek or in English. If you do not read the language of the copy you are asked to sign, have it translated before you sign. The Greek text is the one a court will read if the versions differ and the contract says so.

Notice, inventory and the language of the contract

If you leave early, the notice clause is the one that decides the deposit. Read it twice.

Utilities

Budget for electricity, water, rubbish and the building’s common fund on top of the rent. Electricity is the bill that surprises people, because air-conditioning in July and August is not optional in a glass flat. A modest apartment might see something like €80 to €250 a month at the Electricity Authority of Cyprus, depending on the season, the insulation and how often the units run. That is a range, not a tariff. The tariff is on eac.com.cy. As of 2026, check it.

Water is cheaper than electricity for most households and still worth reading, because a leak or a garden changes it. Municipal rubbish and a sewerage charge appear on a separate bill or inside the common expenses. Internet is a private contract with one of the island’s providers, often €25 to €50 a month for a household connection. None of these is included unless the lease says so.

Put the electricity and water accounts in the name of the person who will live there, once the landlord agrees. An account left in the owner’s name, with the tenant paying the owner in cash, is how disputes start.

Scams and weak files

Most rentals are ordinary. The ones that go wrong follow a pattern.

  • The flat is “available” only if you transfer a deposit before you have been inside it. Do not.
  • The person showing the flat cannot produce the title or a written authority from the owner. Ask for it.
  • The rent is far below the range above, the photos are professional, and the owner is “abroad and cannot meet”. Walk away.
  • You are asked to pay a “reservation” to a personal account in another country. The landlord’s Cyprus account, or the agency’s client account, is the normal destination.
  • A holiday let is described as a long-term home, with a checkout in three months and a ban on registering the address. That will not support a residence file.

A residence application that depends on the address will ask for the lease. A visitor’s file, a digital-nomad file and an employment file all do this in their own way. The lease does not grant the permit. It is one of the papers. The routes are in moving to Cyprus.

Useful services

  • A lawyer, if the rent is high or the landlord is a company, to read the lease. For an ordinary apartment many tenants sign without one. Know which situation you are in.
  • Contents insurance if you own furniture or equipment worth insuring. The building’s policy usually covers the structure, not your laptop.
  • A car, if the flat is outside a centre. Hiring is covered in the car hire guide. Owning is covered in the driving guide.
  • A local bank account if the landlord or a later permit requires one. See opening a bank account.

Questions readers ask

How much is a one-bedroom in 2026?

Asking ranges seen in 2026 are roughly €1,200 to €2,200 in Limassol, €800 to €1,400 in Paphos, €700 to €1,200 in Larnaca and €700 to €1,300 in Nicosia. They are not an index. Check a live listing.

How large is the deposit?

One or two months’ rent is the usual request, often plus the first month in advance. Get a receipt that calls it a deposit and says when it returns.

Are utilities included?

Only if the lease says so. Electricity in summer is the bill that moves, and the tariff is the Electricity Authority’s, not a landlord’s estimate.

What should I refuse?

A transfer before you have seen the flat, a price far below the local range from an owner who cannot meet, and a rent paid to a personal account in another country.

Does the lease give me a residence permit?

No. It is evidence of an address, which several permits ask for. The permit is a separate decision.
