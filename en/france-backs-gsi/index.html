Paris says it fully supports the Great Sea Interconnector

A French foreign ministry spokesman on Monday called the cable strategically important and necessary to link Cyprus to the EU energy network. The survey ship Abeille Marengo has left Heraklion. A professor quoted by Alithia sees the note to Ankara as a dangerous precedent.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

France has publicly backed the Great Sea Interconnector, the cable meant to join the electricity grids of Greece and Cyprus. The Cyprus Mail reports a French foreign ministry spokesman saying on Monday that Paris “fully supported the project’s implementation”, citing its strategic weight and the need to connect Cyprus to the European energy network. Paris described the project as one of European strategic importance.

The same day in Brussels, before the EU Foreign Affairs Council, Greek Foreign Minister Giorgos Gerapetritis said energy links, above all Greece–Cyprus and Greece–Egypt, should go ahead without obstruction. He called for compliance with international law and the EU acquis, and repeated that Greece would not tolerate interference in the GSI. The project, he said, is European in nature and reaches beyond the interests of Cyprus and Greece alone, to Europe, Africa and the Middle East.

The comments followed the departure of the French research ship Abeille Marengo from Heraklion, Crete, to finish outstanding seabed surveys on the first section of the cable. The surveys are complicated by competing Greek and Turkish navigation warnings over the waters the cable is planned to cross.

On Saturday the Greek transmission operator Admie said the Greece–Cyprus section is expected to be finished by the end of 2031. On its latest development plan, about 60 per cent of the required seabed surveys are done.

Separately, Alithia carries an assessment by international-law professor Haris Tzimitras, summarised in StockWatch’s press review. In his view the French verbal note to Turkey about the Abeille Marengo’s work amounts to an indirect permission, sets a precedent Ankara can use, and lends weight to the Turkey–Libya memorandum. He says the text of the note has not been published and was not in the Greek foreign ministry’s briefing to the parties, and he asks whether Athens and Nicosia knew of the step in advance. That is his assessment, not a finding by the paper and not the French ministry’s position. Paris, in the statement the Cyprus Mail reports, speaks of support for implementation, not of handing over rights.
