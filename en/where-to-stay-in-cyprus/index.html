Where to stay in Cyprus: Limassol, Paphos, Ayia Napa and Larnaca

Where to stay in Cyprus: Limassol is the port between Kourion and Amathus, Paphos is UNESCO and PFO airport, Ayia Napa is the Cape Greco resort, Larnaca is Finikoudes and LCA. No hotel rates are printed here.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 19 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 19 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

The Deputy Ministry of Tourism’s 2026 guide does not print a nightly rate and does not pick a “best” city. It describes what each place is, and it names two main airports: Larnaca (LCA) and Paphos (PFO). The comparison below uses only those facts. The Booking button at the end opens a homepage, not a list with today’s price.

Limassol

Lemesos is the largest port, between ancient Amathus to the east and Kourion to the west. The ministry notes the carnival, the winemaking villages and the newer towers on the seafront. Lady’s Mile is called the longest beach on the island. Limassol is not one of the two airports in that list of gateways: the air gateways are Larnaca and Paphos. Limassol port takes most cargo and cruise traffic.

It fits a base for Kourion, Kolossi, Amathus and the city. The ministry’s own day-by-day list is in the things-to-do guide.

Paphos

The whole ancient city of Paphos is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Kato Paphos by the sea sits almost on the Hellenistic city; Pano Paphos is the upper town. Nearby are Petra tou Romiou, the Tombs of the Kings, the mosaics and the castle. The booklet sets the quiet coves of Paphos against the lively shores of Ayia Napa. Coral Bay is described as soft sand and shallow water, with rocky headlands and sea caves to either side. Paphos airport (PFO) is one of the two primary air gateways, and the booklet says both airports are accessible for travellers with disabilities.

It fits an arrival at PFO and days at the mosaics, the Akamas or Polis. Ayia Napa is a longer road from here than from Larnaca: the booklet does not give the kilometres, and this page does not add them.

Ayia Napa

In the booklet this is not an airport city. It is a resort of the Famagusta region, with Paralimni and Protaras: villages that grew into holiday towns, sea and sand. Nissi is the famous bay with an islet you can walk to at low tide. Konnos is a small sandy beach, Cape Greco a little over a mile away, and its trails sit in the same sample day as Ayia Napa monastery and the Thalassa museum. Fig Tree Bay in Protaras is a separate beach, named for a fig tree the text says has grown there since the 17th century.

It fits a trip that is about that coast. The air gateways the booklet names are still Larnaca and Paphos, not a runway in Ayia Napa.

Larnaca

The Finikoudes promenade and its palms, the Church of Saint Lazarus, the Hala Sultan Mosque, Kition and the Pierides Museum. Mackenzie Beach is shallow and, the booklet says, a centre of nightlife. Larnaca airport (LCA) is the other primary air gateway. Larnaca port is smaller than Limassol’s and also takes cargo and passengers. Choirokoitia and Lefkara are on the day trip out of Larnaca, not out of Paphos.

It fits an arrival at LCA and a wish for a seafront town, not only a strip of hotels. Kourion and Paphos are a driving day from here: the rules of that drive are in the car hire guide.

More than 75 beaches on the island carry a Blue Flag. All beaches are public and free. Loungers on municipal beaches are for a fee. That does not argue for one city over another.

Flights and the airport transfer

Larnaca and Paphos are the air gateways in the booklet. Aviasales searches flights into them. Welcome Pickups covers transfers at those two airports, and Kiwitaxi is the wider transfer search. Fares are on their pages, not here.

Questions readers ask

Which cities have the airports?

The 2026 booklet names two primary air gateways: Larnaca (LCA) and Paphos (PFO). Limassol and Ayia Napa are not on that airport list.

Where does the guide put Ayia Napa?

In the Famagusta region, with Paralimni and Protaras, as seaside resorts rather than as the capital.

Are hotel prices on this page?

No. The ministry does not print them in this comparison, and the page does not add any.
