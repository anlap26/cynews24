Parliament unanimously tightens the rules on taking water in Cyprus

On Thursday the House amended the Integrated Water Management Law: periodic reviews of permits, a double fee to license old works, and a fine served within 30 days of detection.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 20 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 20 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Parliament on Thursday unanimously amended the Integrated Water Management Law. Politis and the Cyprus Mail both reported the vote. The change is meant to modernise the rules on water resources and to transpose the European Union’s water framework directive into national law. The directive dates from 2000, four years before Cyprus joined the Union. The Mail wrote that, over the 26 years since, the European Commission had taken the view that Cyprus had not fully implemented it.

The Mail says the Commission threatened last year to take the government to court because Cypriot law did not require periodic reviews of water-abstraction permits. It quoted the Commission: member states must periodically review these controls, including any permits, and update them if need be. “In Cyprus, national legislation does not impose any periodic review, which demonstrates a situation of bad transposition” of the directive, the Commission said, as the Mail reported it.

The amended law requires frequent periodic reviews of the controls on taking surface water and groundwater and on holding surface water back, Politis reported, so that water bodies are not degraded. The water development department can order a stop to abstraction projects that start before a permit is issued, the Mail reported. If an application to license an existing unlicensed work is refused, the director must require that the work be filled in, sealed, closed or destroyed. Politis said that duty was added after concerns in the House agriculture committee, and with the ministry’s agreement.

A work already built without a permit can still be licensed afterwards. The fee is twice the ordinary permit fee. Politis said the application has to be lodged within two years of the law coming into force. Elam MP Sotiris Ioannou moved an amendment that would have replaced that two-year window with a decree of the agriculture minister. The House rejected it. Diko leader Nicholas Papadopoulos said his party backed the bill and voted against the amendment, because without a time limit the question would drag on. Disy MP Giorgos Karaiskakis called the two years reasonable, Politis reported.

An out-of-court fine is set according to the seriousness of the offence and its effect on water resources: the nature, extent and duration of the breach, the size of the intervention, and the condition of the water system. Politis said the fine is served within 30 days of the offence being detected, not of the day it was committed. Interference with a natural watercourse, such as a stream, can bring a requirement to repair the damage.

A representative of the Legal Service told the House that the Republic is in an informal dialogue with the European Commission, and that the hope is the vote will end the infringement procedure, Politis reported. That is a hope stated in the House, not a decision by the Commission.

Papadopoulos said there was no law with a more incorrect name, because the statute is called the Integrated Water Management Law. “In practice, we do not have unified water management because all issues related to water are fragmented into various services and departments. This is a schizophrenic state policy in a country with a very serious water problem,” he said, as the Mail reported. Akel MP Yiannakis Gavriel, who chairs the agriculture committee, said that in a water crisis unlicensed projects undermine the department’s ability to extend its control. Diko MP Zacharias Koulias said that under Spyros Kyprianou, when the dams were built, there was a maintenance team of 40 people, and that only two remain, which he linked to the loss of millions of cubic metres at Mavrokolympos. The Mail notes that it is not true that every reservoir was built under Kyprianou, and that the Mavrokolympos reservoir near Paphos was, in January last year, entirely drained of its 1.4 million cubic metres. Experts said at the time that the pipes, installed in 1966, would have to be replaced.

The vote does not set a single national water price. That separate discussion is here.
