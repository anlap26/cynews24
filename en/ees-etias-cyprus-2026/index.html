EES and ETIAS in 2026, and what they do to a trip to Cyprus

The Entry/Exit System has been fully in use at Schengen borders since 10 April 2026. ETIAS is not open. Official pages do not agree on whether the future authorisation covers Cyprus.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 25 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Two European systems are easy to mix up, and in 2026 only one of them is actually running. The Entry/Exit System, EES, records short stays at the external border. The European Travel Information and Authorisation System, ETIAS, is a permission people would request before travel. They are not visas. They are not residence permits. And they do not both apply, today, to a flight that lands at Larnaca.

EES is the one that is on

The European Commission says EES has been fully operational across the Schengen countries since 10 April 2026. A gradual start began on 12 October 2025 and ran until 9 April 2026. From the April date, the Commission and the EU's own EES pages say the system is in use at external border points of the countries that use it, for non-EU nationals on a short stay: people with a short-stay visa, and people who may stay up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa.

There is no EES application to file before the trip. At the border the check can include a facial image and fingerprints, and the record replaces the old habit of relying only on a stamp. The EU's question page says the progressive period is over. This guide does not describe queues, booth layouts or how long a check takes. Those change by airport and by day, and none of the sources below prints a waiting time for a named Cypriot airport.

EES, on the Commission's description, is a Schengen external-border system. Cyprus is not in the Schengen area. This page therefore does not call the desk at Larnaca or Paphos an EES desk. Whether that changes depends on whether Cyprus joins the area, which is a separate question and is not settled by a press sentence. The state of that question is in Cyprus and Schengen in 2026.

ETIAS is the one that is not open

The Commission's ETIAS page says the system is not in operation and that no applications for travel authorisations are being collected. The Union, it says, will give the start date several months before launch. A news explainer dated 28 April 2026 says ETIAS is scheduled for the last quarter of 2026 and that the exact day will be announced later in the year. "Last quarter of 2026" is a plan in that explainer, not a day this guide can turn into a booking rule. This page was updated on 25 November 2026. If a start date has since been posted on the Commission page, that page wins.

The same ETIAS page says the fee has been set at €20 instead of the earlier €7, that the change was in a review by the Council and the Parliament, and that the fee takes effect when ETIAS is operational. Nobody is paying it to the official system today, because the system is not taking applications. The Commission says people will apply on an official website or app, and it warns that other sites may charge extra or misuse personal data. A page that offers "ETIAS approved" in 2026, before the Commission has opened the system, is not the procedure described here.

When it does open, the April explainer says an authorisation is linked to the passport, lasts three years or until the passport expires, whichever is sooner, and most applications in the Commission's description are approved within minutes. It also says approval is needed before boarding. Those are statements about a future system. They are not a promise about a trip this month.

Cyprus is where the official pages split

The April 2026 Commission explainer says ETIAS will change short stays in the Schengen area and in Cyprus, and it counts 30 countries. The French foreign ministry's page, updated on 10 November 2026, still describes ETIAS as applying to EU member states except Ireland and Cyprus, plus the Schengen associated countries. Both pages are official. They do not say the same thing about Cyprus. This guide does not pick the one that is more convenient. Before a visa-free trip, read the Commission's ETIAS page again. If it still says applications are not collected, there is nothing to buy.

A national of a country that needs a short-stay visa for the Schengen area does not, on the Commission's April explainer, also need an ETIAS. The explainer uses India as that example: the visa is the permission, and EES is the record at the Schengen border. Visa-free examples, such as the United States in that same explainer, are the travellers ETIAS is aimed at. Your passport may be neither example. The Commission's country list, when the system opens, is the list that matters.

Questions readers ask

Is ETIAS open in 2026?

No. The European Commission says ETIAS is not in operation and that no applications are being collected. It has said the start is planned for the last quarter of 2026 and that the exact date will be announced months ahead. A site that takes an application now is not the official system.

What does EES do?

At a Schengen external border it records a short stay by a non-EU national, including a facial image and fingerprints, instead of relying only on a passport stamp. The Commission says it has been fully operational since 10 April 2026. You do not apply for EES in advance.

Does EES run at Larnaca and Paphos?

The Commission's description of EES is the external border of the Schengen countries. Cyprus is not in Schengen. This guide does not describe the Larnaca desk as an EES desk. See the Schengen guide for the status of the island.

Will ETIAS cover Cyprus?

A Commission explainer of 28 April 2026 says ETIAS will cover short stays in the Schengen area and Cyprus, 30 countries. A French foreign-ministry page updated on 10 November 2026 still says ETIAS is for EU states except Ireland and Cyprus. The two pages disagree. Check the official ETIAS page before you travel.

How much is the ETIAS fee?

The Commission says the fee has been set at €20, replacing the earlier €7, and that it takes effect when ETIAS is operational. It is not being collected now. Exemptions are described on the Commission page and can include younger and older applicants. Do not pay a third-party site.
