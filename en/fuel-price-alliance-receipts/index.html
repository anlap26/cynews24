Christodoulides asks companies to cut prices as MPs extend the fuel-tax cut to March 2027 and order a tax breakdown on receipts

The president called EKO’s heating-fuel discount an act of responsibility and urged other firms to follow. Parliament extended the reduced excise duty to 31 March 2027 and will make stations itemise taxes from 1 February.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 20 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 20 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

President Nikos Christodoulides on Thursday welcomed fuel retailer EKO’s decision to cut kerosene and heating-oil prices at its stations. Cyprus Mail quoted him calling it a “tangible act of responsibility” at “a time when families need it most”. He asked every company in the sector to “consider similar initiatives” and to “make a practical contribution towards easing the burden on the public”. “The high cost of living cannot be addressed solely through state decisions. It requires an alliance of responsibility in which everyone contributes their fair share,” he said.

According to the same report, the president said the government has “been doing precisely that since day one” and has been “acting with a clear plan, rather than through improvisation or populism”. “We have already allocated over €1 billion to support households, workers and businesses,” he said, adding that a few weeks ago the government approved a new package that increased support by a further €160 million. Philenews separately reported that he foreshadowed new support measures ahead of Christmas without giving details, and that he put existing support above €1 billion. The paper described three fronts at once: the government’s appeal, parliament’s decisions and the oil companies’ response. October’s inflation figures are in the price-index article.

EKO itself, Cyprus Mail reported, is offering households up to 10 per cent off heating oil and kerosene, plus a separate benefit on transport fuels, to ease family costs over the winter and the festive period. The company said orders of heating oil and kerosene placed through EKO stations get a 5 per cent reduction. Customers who also use the EKO Smile app receive another 5 per cent, taking the saving to as much as 10 per cent. Philenews said this initiative was the occasion for the president’s appeal.

Other companies’ reaction was mixed, Philenews reported. Petrolina chief executive Dinos Lefkaritis called the president’s statement unfair and misplaced, saying every company announces offer packages from time to time. He pointed to the daily price observatory and said prices sit at roughly the same level, which he argued shows that competition is working. “Every company is entitled to advertise,” he said. Esso executive director Natasa Pileidou said all companies are trying to cut prices and run promotions because they recognise the pressure energy costs put on households and businesses, and that prices are falling across companies precisely because competition works.

On the same Thursday, parliament unanimously extended the reduced excise duty on motor fuel until 31 March 2027. Philenews described the measure as a cut of 8.33 cents a litre. Cyprus Mail gave the rates with VAT included: petrol from €0.429 to €0.359 a litre, and diesel from €0.40 to €0.33. The government estimates the lower tax take for the period up to March at about €25 million, Cyprus Mail reported. The discounted rates had been due to expire at the end of November. MPs recalled that excise duty is already at the lowest level the EU allows, while the government is talking to the European Commission about a possible further cut as an exemption. Philenews added that the parties welcomed Finance Minister Makis Keravnos’s approaches to Brussels to zero the consumption tax on heating oil and to abolish double taxation on fuel. The Commission has not yet replied.

Akel and Elam asked for more support. Akel MP Aristos Damianou said, Philenews reported, that the state has taken in an extra €538 million from the rise in prices, and he accused the government of weak social reflexes. Disy MP Savia Orphanidou welcomed the extension but said the government still owes the country a coherent long-term energy policy, and she criticised how spending is prioritised. Elam’s Marios Pelekanos welcomed the finance minister’s handling and at the same time pressed for support for lower-middle incomes, large families, single-parent families and low-income pensioners.

A separate law, tabled by Disy MP and energy-committee chair Nikos Georgiou, will force stations to show how the price is built. Philenews, and a headline carried by Economy Today, put the start date at 1 February 2027; Cyprus Mail said the requirement kicks in as of 1 February. Receipts and invoices will list, separately, excise duty, VAT, the contribution to the Cyprus Organisation for the Management of Petroleum Stocks, and the amount left after those charges. Georgiou said that, with the cost of living high, a buyer should be able to see how much is the fuel and how much is tax. Both he and Philenews noted that transparency on its own does not bring prices down. Akel MP Andreas Pashiourtidis said, Cyprus Mail reported, that station owners should welcome the measure, because it would show that when prices are higher the state collects more tax. Alma’s Irini Charalambidou recalled that, in a recent discussion, officials could not produce figures for tax revenue from motor fuel. The paper said the breakdown should also help drivers see which stations are applying the reduced rate, after reports that some outlets had not lowered their prices.

Christodoulos Christodoulou, spokesman for the petrol-station owners’ association, told Philenews that, following the fall in international prices, 95-octane petrol has dropped by 16 cents a litre since 22 October, motor diesel by 4 cents and heating oil by 8 cents. The same report said Brent was around $92 a barrel on Thursday, below previous weeks when it had reached $115.
