Moving to Cyprus: residence routes, tax residency and the first months

There is no statute called the first 90 days. The clocks are the ones departments publish: four months for MEU1, a visitor permit before the stay runs out, and a tax test that a residence card does not settle.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 27 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

People who look up a move to Cyprus often ask for a list of the first 90 days, as if a statute had that name. It does not. The clocks that exist are the ones the departments publish, and they are not the same clock for every passport. This guide puts the residence routes, the rules of stay and the tax tests in that order. It is not a permit, a tax return or a quote.

What to sort in the first months

The practical sequence is short, and most of it waits on a document you can show.

  • A place to live that you can prove, usually a lease or a title, because later forms ask for it.
  • The residence step that matches the passport: MEU1 for a Union citizen, a visitor permit for many third-country nationals who are not working, or a different permit if the purpose is work, study or remote work for a foreign employer.
  • A tax identification code once it is clear a return will have to be filed. Arrival by itself does not create the duty to file, and a yellow slip is not a reason to skip a return that is due.
  • Social insurance only if the person will be gainfully occupied. Simply landing does not issue a number.
  • A payment account. Some files later ask for a Cyprus account. An account with an EU payment service is not automatically that account.
  • GeSY once the migration record can identify a non-Cypriot who holds a valid residence permit. A visitor who is still on a stamp is not there yet.
  • Electricity and water in the name of the person who is allowed to occupy the home.
  • A driving-licence exchange only after the Road Transport Department’s residence test, which is at least 185 days of normal residence, not the day you collect the keys.

A pink slip is not permission to work. A contract of employment needs its own permit when the worker is a third-country national. The order, and a sister site’s notes on what not to file early, starts at MyCyTax.

The residence routes

The route depends on the passport and on what the person will actually do. Mixing the checklists is the usual way a file comes back.

Citizens of the EU, the EEA and Switzerland

A citizen of the European Union, the European Economic Area or Switzerland who will stay on applies for the MEU1 registration certificate. The Migration Department says the application goes in within four months of entry. The department’s fee on that page is €20. The certificate does not expire, and the holder may work. Family members who are themselves Union citizens use the same route. Family members who are not need the family-member procedure on the same set of pages, not the visitor form.

The page to read is the department’s note on the registration certificate MEU1. As of 2026, check that page before you rely on a fee or a deadline copied into a forum post.

Visitors who are not working

A third-country national who is visiting, and not working, uses the visitor procedure. The form is MVIS8. The department’s forms page is the visitor forms list, and the narrative page is visitors and family members. The application has to be in before the permission to stay runs out. The permit is the item people call a pink slip. It does not allow economic activity.

The supporting list, in the version widely quoted from February 2026, asks for foreign income of at least €2,000 a month or €24,000 a year for one person, shown as money arriving in the applicant’s name, plus 20 percent for a spouse and 15 percent for each child. It also asks for accommodation, medical insurance, medical tests on a first filing, and a bank guarantee whose amount depends on the country of origin. Download the current list from the forms page. The file is revised, and a blog’s table is not the list the officer will use.

Work, study and remote work

Employment for a third-country national is a different permit, tied to an employer and to quotas the ministry publishes. It is not a visitor file with a contract stapled to it. Study is another file. Remote work for an employer or for clients outside Cyprus is the digital nomad scheme, which is open again with a cap of 500 permits and a net-income test of at least €3,500 a month. That scheme has its own page and its own list of papers. A yellow-slip checklist will not produce a pink slip, and a nomad file is not examined as a visitor application.

Permanent residence from €300,000

A further route is the immigration permit under Regulation 6(2), for a third-country national who invests at least €300,000 on the criteria the Migration Department publishes. A house or apartment has to be a first sale from a developer, at least €300,000 plus VAT. Other property, company shares and certain fund units are separate categories, and resale is allowed in some of those categories but not in the house category. The applicant also shows a secure annual income of at least €50,000, plus €15,000 for a spouse and €10,000 for each dependent minor child. For a house or apartment that income has to come from abroad. The permit is not citizenship and it is not tax residence. The criteria are on the department’s investor page. As of 2026, check that page: the department has revised the criteria before, and an agent’s PDF from 2021 is not the current text.

Rules of stay

Cyprus is not a Schengen member. The 90-days-in-180 rule that people know from Schengen travel is not the rule that governs a stay in the Republic. A short visit lasts for the period the stamp or the visa gives. Overstaying and then filing is a worse file than filing while the stay is still lawful. For a longer stay, the lawful step is the one in the table above, chosen for the passport and the purpose.

The Republic’s visa list is aligned with the Schengen list in many cases, so someone who needs a Schengen visa often needs a Cyprus visa as well. Alignment is not membership. Count the days on the Cyprus stamp.

Tax residence is a separate test

A residence card does not make a person tax resident. The Tax Department applies the Income Tax Law. An individual is tax resident under the 183-day rule or, if every part of it is met, the 60-day rule. The department’s page is tax residency and domicile.

The 183-day rule

Stay in Cyprus for one or more periods that together exceed 183 days in the tax year. The day of arrival counts as a day in Cyprus. The day of departure counts as a day outside Cyprus. Arrival and departure on the same day count as a day in Cyprus. Departure and arrival on the same day count as a day outside Cyprus. The year is the calendar year.

The 60-day rule

All four conditions have to be true. The person is in Cyprus for at least 60 days. The person is not resident for more than 183 days in any other single country. The person carries on a business in Cyprus, or is employed in Cyprus, or holds an office in Cyprus. The person maintains a permanent home in Cyprus, owned or rented. If the business stops or the employment ends during the year, the person ceases to be tax resident for that year under this rule. A rented flat with no activity in Cyprus does not complete the test. Sixty days on the island, without the other three facts, does not either.

From the 2026 tax year, a tax resident aged 25 to 70 files a return even with no income. That is a filing rule. It is not a budget, and it is not created by the residence card. The bands, the non-dom exemption from Special Defence Contribution, and a worked example belong on the tax guide and in the MyCyTax calculators.

Money, housing and the pages around this one

Cost and tax are two bills. One is rent or a purchase. The other is income tax, and only once the residence tests are met. This page does not print a single monthly cost for Limassol or Paphos, because no official source publishes one figure called “moving to Cyprus”. Ranges, and what they leave out, are in the cost of living guide. The cities are compared in where to live.

Buying and renting are different files. A lease is the usual proof of address for a visitor or a new employee. A purchase can support an investment permit only when the property matches the category. Neither document is a title deed by itself. The purchase sequence is in buying property. The lease, the deposit and the usual scams are in renting an apartment.

Questions readers ask

Is there a legal checklist called the first 90 days?

No. The deadlines that exist are the ones each department publishes. A Union citizen applies for MEU1 within four months of entry. A visitor files before the permission to stay runs out. The tax year is a separate count.

Does a residence card make me tax resident?

No. Tax residence is the 183-day rule or the 60-day rule, both stated by the Tax Department. The 60-day rule needs a home in Cyprus and a business, employment or office in Cyprus, and it fails if that activity stops during the year.

Can I work on a visitor permit?

No. The visitor permit is for a person who is not carrying on an economic activity. A job needs an employment permit. Remote work for a foreign employer is the digital nomad scheme, if you qualify.

Is the Schengen 90-in-180 rule the rule in Cyprus?

No. Cyprus is not a Schengen member. Count the days on the Cyprus stamp or visa. The visa lists are aligned in many cases, which is not the same thing as membership.
