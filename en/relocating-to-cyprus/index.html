Moving to Cyprus: the first months, the residence routes, and the tax

There is no statute called the first 90 days. The clocks are the ones departments publish: four months for MEU1, a pink slip before the visa expires, a driving-licence exchange after 185 days. A residence card does not make a person tax resident.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

People looking up a move to Cyprus often ask for the first 90 days, as if a statute listed them. There is no law by that name. The clocks that do exist are the ones the departments publish, and a relocation guide puts the first months in the order those deadlines impose, then separates the residence routes from the tax questions. Nothing in it is a permit, a tax assessment or a quote.

The first months are a sequence, not a homemade timetable. A Union citizen who will stay on applies for the MEU1 registration certificate within four months of entry. The Migration Department’s fee is €20. A third-country visitor applies for the MVIS8 permit before the visa expires. Around those clocks sit a lease or a title, which later forms ask for; a tax identification code once a return must be filed; social insurance only if the person will be gainfully occupied, since arrival alone issues no number; a payment account; GESY once the migration registry can identify a non-Cypriot who holds a valid residence permit; electricity and water in the occupier’s name; and a driving-licence exchange only after at least 185 days of normal residence, the Road Transport Department’s test. A yellow slip is not a reason to skip the tax return. A pink slip is not permission to work. The order, and what not to file early: https://mycytax.com/guides/relocation/first-90-days/?utm_source=cynews24&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=guides

The route depends on the passport and on what the person will do. A citizen of the European Union, the European Economic Area or Switzerland uses MEU1. The certificate does not expire, and the holder may work. A third-country national who is visiting, and not working, uses MVIS8 and cannot carry out an economic activity. Employment for a third-country national is a different permit, not a visitor file. A third route is the immigration permit under Regulation 6(2), tied to an investment on the criteria the Migration Department publishes. It is not citizenship and it is not tax residence. A yellow-slip checklist will not produce a pink slip, and an investment file is not examined as a visitor application. The three routes side by side: https://mycytax.com/guides/relocation/residence-options/?utm_source=cynews24&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=guides

Cost and tax are two bills, and they should not be added together. One is the cost of buying or renting a home. The other is income tax, and only once the Tax Department’s residence tests are met. A residence card does not create tax residence. From the 2026 tax year a resident aged 25 to 70 files a return even with no income. That is a filing rule, not a budget. The relocation page does not print a monthly cost of living for Limassol or Paphos, because no official source it relies on publishes one figure for moving to Cyprus. Where a figure does exist, the page points onward, to the September 2026 comparison of housing-loan rates and to the resident handbook for each registration.
