Cyprus is producing copper again: Hellenic Cuprum turns out 99.999% pure cathodes

SXEW Hellenic Cuprum is operating at the Skouriotissa and Apliki mines, exporting all of its output, and since June 2026 has covered a substantial share of its power use with its own solar plant.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 16 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 16 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Copper has been bound up with Cyprus since antiquity: even the Latin name of the metal, cuprum, comes from the Roman aes Cyprium, “copper of Cyprus,” Philenews recalls. Industrial production is under way on the island again. The paper’s journalists visited the Skouriotissa and Apliki mines, at the foot of the Marathasa area, where SXEW Hellenic Cuprum Ltd is operating.

The company uses SX-EW technology — solvent extraction and electrowinning. This hydrometallurgical process yields high-purity copper without conventional smelting. According to Philenews, the plant produces copper cathodes of 99.999% purity, meeting the London Metal Exchange’s Grade A specification. All of the output is exported.

Since June 2026 a substantial share of the company’s electricity demand has been met by renewable generation installed within its mining concession: a 2.5 MW photovoltaic park and a 5 MWh energy-storage system. The facilities, the paper writes, were built on land that required environmental rehabilitation and were financed entirely with private funds, without a government grant. Planning began in 2023, construction in August 2024, and copper production and exports in May 2025. The company says it is already close to meeting all of its electricity needs from independent renewable sources and considers that goal feasible.

The environmental approach described by Philenews includes recovering material left from earlier mining as well as newly extracted ore, reusing equipment where that is technically possible, plans for a facility to recycle scrap copper, and work to restore land affected by previous operations. Global demand for copper is rising because of electric vehicles, electricity networks, solar panels, storage systems and artificial-intelligence infrastructure; the company, the paper notes, aims to contribute to Europe’s supply of strategic raw materials and to Cypriot exports.
