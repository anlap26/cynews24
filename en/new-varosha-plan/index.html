Nicosia condemns Ustel's "new Varosha" and takes it to the United Nations

On Monday he said the plan was ready and would change occupied Famagusta. On Tuesday Letymbiotis said the fenced city is governed by resolutions 550 and 789, and unilateral steps are not allowed.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

On Monday the so-called "prime minister" in the north, Unal Ustel, announced that a "New Varosha vision plan" was ready. Turkish Cypriot media, as carried by Sigmalive and Politis citing the Cyprus News Agency, reported that he called it on social media the largest structural project they had prepared, after promises of a "free university" and buyable military service. Details, he said, would be published shortly. The area, he said, would become one of the stronger tourist destinations. He linked the scheme to the 50th anniversary, in 2033, of the declaration of the unrecognised entity, and jabbed his rivals for copying the slogans of the National Unity Party.

Earlier the same day, on television, Ustel rejected President Nikos Christodoulides's proposal to place the fenced city under European Union control, and said Varosha's future should be discussed as part of an overall settlement. He also said the hotels in the closed city had reached the end of their life: seismic checks, he said, showed the buildings examined were not earthquake-resistant, and some were already starting to collapse and were a danger to their surroundings. Those are his claims, as reported by Turkish Cypriot media, not a finding by the Republic's authorities.

On Tuesday government spokesman Konstantinos Letymbiotis said the government was assessing the reports of the plan "with due seriousness". No specific details had been published so far, he told Kathimerini and the agency. "We want to be absolutely clear: the status of the fenced city of Famagusta is governed by the relevant United Nations Security Council resolutions, 550 and 789, and it does not admit any unilateral action or any action that violates those resolutions."

Letymbiotis recalled the point in the president's 19 proposals to the Turkish Cypriot leader that deals specifically with the fenced area of Famagusta and provides for the city's port to be opened at the same time, under the European Commission. The government, he said, was weighing the facts and the moment, including the so-called "elections" expected in the occupied areas in December. "But in no case can we downgrade or sideline references of this kind. We condemn them and we proceed with all the necessary diplomatic steps with all our partners and with the United Nations." The government, he added, would keep acting on the basis of international law and pressing for what the Security Council resolutions require.
