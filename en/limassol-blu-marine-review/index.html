Limassol Blu Marine: what Leptos publishes about the seafront towers

An independent Limassol Blu Marine review. This is not a listing. A Poseidon studio on the project page is €658,000 plus VAT. A June 2026 company text said Cavalli “from €845,000”.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 21 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 21 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

This is an independent reading of public texts on Limassol Blu Marine, on the Limassol seafront. The developer is Leptos Estates, part of the Leptos Group. CyNews 24 is not selling these homes and has no partnership with Leptos. What follows is what the company published itself or placed in a press release. What those pages do not say is not filled in here.

The project page on leptosestates.com describes a seafront residential scheme and names the British practices Benoy and BurroHapold. That is how the second name is spelled on the page; the firm is otherwise known as Buro Happold. The text promises a podium 9 metres up, with a gym, a lobby, a bistro, a restaurant, a spa and a 50-metre outdoor pool, plus underground parking and EV charging. Times on the same page: 49 minutes to the airport, 1 minute to the sea, 2 minutes to shops, 9 to healthcare and 12 to schools. Those are the company’s minutes, not a measured distance. The page we read gives no street number. A Leptos release of 30 July 2025 places Cavalli Tower in central Limassol, with access to the marina and the business district.

A press release that the Cyprus Mail ran on 10 June 2026, labelled Press Release, speaks of an investment of more than €500 million and of four towers, two residential and two office. It says both office towers were “sold exclusively”, and that Poseidon, called the first residential tower, is about 95 per cent sold and nearing completion. Those are the company’s words in someone else’s news slot, not an inspection and not an audited figure.

The same text calls Cavalli Tower the first Roberto Cavalli-branded residences in Europe and gives a start of €845,000 for one- to five-bedroom apartments. Leptos’s own release of 30 July 2025 draws the brand geography differently: the first Cavalli project of this kind in the Mediterranean, not “in Europe”. That release also describes a 30-storey tower, a collaboration of about €200 million, completion expected within three years of the release date, and calls the tower the second and final residential building. Neither text gives a storey count for Poseidon. The July 2025 amenity list names 1,150 m² of wellness space, a 25-metre indoor pool, a cinema, a golf simulator, a business centre and gardens covering 70% of the project. A line “Award-winning Architecture” on the project page names no prize, and no award is repeated here.

The prices on the project page on the day of this reading are different. Poseidon studio 205: 45.5 m², €658,000 plus VAT. Studio 206: 70.65 m², €680,000 plus VAT. Apartment 207: 111.35 m², €870,000 plus VAT. On the same page, Cavalli three-bedroom 505 is 213.39 m² at €1,425,000 plus VAT, two-bedroom 202 is 183.77 m² at €1,704,000 plus VAT, and three-bedroom 501 is 143 m² at €1,955,000 plus VAT. The June “from €845,000” and these cards are not one number. Cards change. A buyer looks at the unit on offer now, not at a press-release line.

What the published record supports: a named developer, a seafront position, a split between Poseidon and Cavalli, and covered areas with “plus VAT” on specific cards. What it does not settle: 95 per cent and “nearing completion” are a company statement, “in Europe” and “in the Mediterranean” do not match, Poseidon has no completion year on the project page, and “within three years” is tied to July 2025 rather than a date in a contract.

The scheme suits a buyer who wants an apartment on central Limassol’s seafront and will tell the earlier Poseidon tower from Cavalli, which the company still described as under construction in 2025. The office towers are a different product: the June text says they were sold. It is a poor fit for someone looking for a house in the hills, or for a price near the reduced-VAT ceiling.

Before a contract, check the area, the floor, and whether Cavalli furniture is included: the June text calls the furniture package optional, not standard. PwC’s Cyprus summary puts ordinary VAT at 19%. It describes the 5% rate as the first 130 m², up to a value of €350,000, if total buildable area is not over 190 m², the transaction is not over €475,000, and the home stays the main residence for ten years. This is not tax advice. The Department of Lands and Surveys says a transfer that is subject to VAT pays no transfer fee, and a transfer that is not subject to VAT gets a 50% reduction of that fee. The Tax Department sets capital gains tax at 20% on a profit from disposing of immovable property in Cyprus, with listed exemptions. The published “from €658,000” and “from €845,000” sit above the €475,000 transaction ceiling in that summary. Ask what share of Poseidon is still for sale, which date is in a Cavalli contract, and what the service charge covers. The form under this article goes to CyNews 24, not to Leptos.

Questions readers ask

What is the entry price at Blu Marine?

On the project page the day this was written, the lowest card was a Poseidon studio at €658,000 plus VAT. A press release of 10 June 2026 said Cavalli from €845,000. The Cavalli cards on the page were higher. The list can change.

Is Poseidon finished?

A company text of 10 June 2026 says Poseidon is about 95 per cent sold and nearing completion. That is not an inspection and not a contract date. The project page does not print a completion year.

When is Cavalli due?

Leptos’s release of 30 July 2025 says construction is under way and completion was expected within three years of that date. A calendar year in a contract does not follow from that sentence. Check with the developer.

Is this a Leptos listing?

No. It is a reading of public pages and press releases. The form under the text goes to CyNews 24.
