44-year-old woman found dead in Nicosia as police search for a third person; flat reportedly used as a massage parlour

Police are treating the case as suspected premeditated murder and attempted murder after a second woman was found injured at a nearby junction.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 16 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 16 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Cyprus police are investigating a suspected premeditated murder and attempted murder after two women were found injured in Nicosia late on Saturday, 14 November. According to the police, a report came in at around 11.30pm of an injured woman at the junction of Digenis Akritas and Larnakos avenues.

Officers who arrived found a woman of Asian origin with wounds to her neck and back. She was taken by ambulance to Nicosia General Hospital, where she underwent surgery. On Sunday, Cyprus Mail, citing the police, said she remained in a critical condition. Later reports by Philenews and Cyprus Times described her condition by Monday morning as serious but no longer life-threatening; Cyprus Times said she was in the intensive care unit.

A further search of the area found bloodstains outside a nearby block of flats, which led officers to one of the apartments. Inside they found another woman unconscious, with injuries to her neck and head. Forensic pathologists Angeliki Papetta and Orthodoxos Orthodoxou confirmed her death at the scene, Politis reported. Police say the woman who died was a 44-year-old Vietnamese national. Neither woman's name has been officially released.

Nicosia Criminal Investigation Department is leading the inquiry. According to information published by Philenews, the flat where the dead woman was found — the paper locates it in the Agios Antonios area — reportedly operated as a massage parlour. Police are searching for a third person in connection with the case and, the same report says, have not ruled out looking at someone who may have visited the premises as a customer. Both scenes remain under police guard, Politis reported.

This account reflects information published by the morning of Monday, 16 November. The investigation is continuing.
