Dream Tower, Agios Tychonas: what the developer and agents actually publish

An independent reading of Dream Tower in Limassol. This is not a sale and not a partnership. The developer page starts at €890,000 and says 150 m to the sea. Agents publish other figures.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 20 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 20 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

This is an independent reading of public texts on Dream Tower in Agios Tychonas, Limassol. CyNews 24 is not selling these homes and has no partnership with bbf. Figures below come from the developer, a news report, or an agency. They can change. VAT is extra only where a source says so.

The project page says a 27-storey tower on Limassol’s tourist seafront, with one- to four-bedroom homes, duplexes and penthouses, floor-to-ceiling glass, and four levels of amenities. The summary line says 150 m to the sea, a start of €890,000, and energy class A. The area is named Agios Tychonas. The amenity list names a pool, a gym, a sauna, tennis and padel, and a children’s playground. That page does not give a residence count or floor areas. It also gives no completion year.

On 3 October 2026 Cyprus Business News reported an announcement: bbf had formed a collaboration with HKCY Hotels Ltd and added Dream Tower to its sales portfolio. The report, quoting the announcement, describes a 27-storey building 150 metres from the sea, more than 90% of homes with a direct sea view and the rest with mountain views, underground parking, a 24-hour concierge, four amenity floors, indoor and outdoor pools, a spa, a hammam, a sauna, and residents-only courts. Those are the company’s words in someone else’s news story, not an inspection of the building.

Agencies do not match each other. Antaria Homes writes “from €990,000”, 38 residences, areas of 57–274 m², a status of Ready, and also a handover in the second quarter of 2026. Abuda writes “from €990,000 plus VAT”, also 38 residences, and describes areas up to about 506 m², calling the building ready to move into. A ceiling near €6.96 million, and a 2024 completion, are not taken from these pages as an agreed fact. The developer page does not choose between the agent versions.

What the record supports: the address, the storey count, the amenity list and a starting price are on the developer’s page, and a news report records who is selling it. What it does not settle: €890,000 and the agents’ “from €990,000” are different numbers, the areas do not match, and one agent card says Ready and a 2026 handover at the same time.

Before a contract, the specification of one apartment matters more than a slogan. PwC’s Cyprus summary puts ordinary VAT at 19%. It describes the 5% rate as the first 130 m², up to a value of €350,000, if total buildable area is not over 190 m², the transaction is not over €475,000, and the home stays the main residence for ten years. The published starting prices are above that transaction ceiling. This is not tax advice. The Department of Lands and Surveys says a VAT transfer pays no transfer fee, and a non-VAT transfer gets a 50% reduction of that fee. The Tax Department sets capital gains tax at 20% on a profit from disposing of Cyprus immovable property, with listed exemptions.

Source for the building itself: the developer website. The collaboration report: Cyprus Business News, 3 October 2026. The price and area disagreements: Antaria Homes and Abuda. The form under this article goes to CyNews 24, not to the developer.

Questions readers ask

Is the building finished?

The developer page and the 3 October 2026 news report do not give a completion year. Antaria marks it Ready and also lists a second-quarter 2026 handover. Abuda calls it ready to move into. Those texts do not share one date.

What is the entry price?

The developer page says €890,000 and does not mention VAT. Antaria says from €990,000. Abuda says from €990,000 plus VAT.

How many homes, and what size?

The developer page does not publish a count or floor areas. Antaria says 38 residences and 57–274 m². Abuda also says 38 and describes areas up to about 506 m².

No. The form under the article sends the question to CyNews 24. It does not go to the developer.
