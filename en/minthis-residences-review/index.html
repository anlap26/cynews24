Minthis Residences: what Pafilia publishes about the homes at Minthis Hills

An independent Minthis Residences review. This is not a listing. Pafilia’s page gives homes of 220–333 m² and energy class A. It states no sale price and no home count.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 21 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 21 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

This is an independent reading of Pafilia’s public pages on Minthis Residences at Tsada, near Paphos. CyNews 24 is not selling these homes and has no partnership with Pafilia. What follows is only what the developer wrote. A nightly rate on a stay page, if one is printed there, is not a purchase price. VAT is mentioned only where a source names it. No sale price is named here.

The collection page says the homes take their cue from traditional Cypriot houses, with large windows, natural materials, terraces and courtyards. The figures on that page: a minimum of three to four bedrooms, 220–333 m², and energy class A. The named types are Petra, Electra, Callisto, Amalthia, Cleone, Artemis, Asteria, Harmonia, Atlas and Maia. Petra’s living room opens to a pool. Electra puts the main bedroom and the pool on the main level, with guest rooms below. Those are layout descriptions, not a price list. The page says selected homes can add a gym, a sauna, a guest house or a wine cellar. In its own questions and answers, Pafilia says the extras depend on the individual home, its specification and the stage of construction. The same answers tell a buyer to look at ownership costs and access to the facilities, and to take independent legal advice. There is no home count. There is no completion year for a given phase. There is no sale price. This article therefore says price on request, rather than a number.

The address on the community and contact pages is Tsada, 8540, Paphos. Pafilia says the place is 10 minutes from Paphos, in the wine-producing area, at 550 metres. The contact page adds about 20 minutes to the nearest shore and to Paphos airport, and about 40 minutes to Limassol. Those are the site’s times, not a peak-hour measurement.

The land the community page describes is wider than one collection of houses. Pafilia writes of 5 million m² inside a Natura 2000 area, of which 3% is to be built on, and of an 18-hole golf course beside a 12th-century monastery. It names Woods Bagot as architects and WATG for the master plan. On the course, the page both says Mackenzie & Ebert designed it and says Donald Steel designed it in 1994 and Mackenzie & Ebert later renovated it. Both sentences are on that page. What matters more than the slogan is that Residences are not the only product. The same page lists separate lines: Topos, villas, two-bedroom suites with a shared pool, and the Lofos neighbourhood of villas, apartments and duplexes. A home in one line should not be read as a home in another.

The timeline on the community page: opening in 2014, the course in 2015, a first neighbourhood in 2016, villas and suites launched in 2017, Topos in 2020, accommodation in 2021, the square and the spa in 2022. That is Pafilia’s history of the place, not the key date for a house on sale today. Because the answers still mention a “stage of construction”, some homes can still be altered and some may not be built yet. Availability is a question for the developer.

What the published record supports: Pafilia’s name, a Tsada location, sizes, class A, named plans, and a clear split between products. What it does not settle: no sale price, no home count, no completion date for a given unit, and a phrase about awards on the community page, in the text we read, names no prize. The Natura 2000 designation and the 3% limit are Pafilia’s statements. What can still be built is a question for the permit, not for a paragraph on the site.

Minthis Residences suits a buyer who wants a house in the hills near Paphos, with a golf course and the services of the community, and who accepts a drive to the sea that the site puts at about 20 minutes. It is a poor fit for someone who wants an apartment on the Limassol seafront, or a price that can be checked against a list on the website.

Before a contract, ask for the price of one type, what the 220–333 m² includes, which extras are still possible, the payment schedule, and whether there is a separate community charge. PwC’s Cyprus summary puts ordinary VAT at 19%. It describes the 5% rate as the first 130 m², up to a value of €350,000, if total buildable area is not over 190 m², the transaction is not over €475,000, and the home stays the main residence for ten years. This is not tax advice. The Department of Lands and Surveys says a transfer that is subject to VAT pays no transfer fee, and a transfer that is not subject to VAT gets a 50% reduction of that fee. The Tax Department sets capital gains tax at 20% on a profit from disposing of immovable property in Cyprus, with listed exemptions. An interior of 220–333 m² is not the same measure as the buildable area in the 5% description, which caps that area at 190 m². Which rate applies to the contract is a question for the Tax Department, not for this page. The form under this article goes to CyNews 24, not to Pafilia.

Questions readers ask

What do Minthis Residences cost?

The collection page states no sale price. A nightly rate on a stay page, if one is printed, is not a purchase price. We do not add a figure: the price is on request from the developer.

How many homes, and when is completion?

The page publishes neither a home count nor a completion year for a given unit. Pafilia’s answers say extras depend on the stage of construction. Check current availability with the developer.

Is this the same as the villas or Lofos?

No. The community page lists Residences, Topos, villas, suites and Lofos as separate lines. Suites are described there as two-bedroom. The Residences page gives a minimum of three to four bedrooms.

Is this a Pafilia listing?

No. The form under the article goes to CyNews 24, not to the developer.
