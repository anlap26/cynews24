Cabinet weighs a single Cyprus water tariff, with €10 a cubic metre above 81

The interior ministry wants one scale for the whole island, caps on sewerage and stormwater fees, and a lower band for vulnerable households. The cabinet had not voted on Tuesday morning, and parliament would still have to approve the texts.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 17 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

On Tuesday the cabinet is due to consider a package that would, for the first time, set a single water charge across Cyprus. Phileleftheros says the interior ministry prepared it after talks with the district local-government organisations, the agriculture ministry and the Water Development Department. The English-language Philenews names Interior Minister Constantinos Ioannou as the minister asking the cabinet to approve the regulations and an amending bill, and to authorise him to send them to the House.

Before the local-government reform, water and sewerage were billed by separate water boards, sewerage boards, municipalities and community councils. The bill depended on the district and the village. The new text sets one price per cubic metre for every consumer, common caps on sewerage and stormwater fees, and a lower scale for vulnerable groups, including large families.

For an ordinary household Phileleftheros sets out this monthly scale: a €4 standing charge; €1.05 per cubic metre for the first 10; €1.45 from the 11th to the 20th; €1.80 from the 21st to the 30th; €2.50 from the 31st to the 40th; €3 from the 41st to the 60th; €5 from the 61st to the 80th. Above 81 cubic metres the rate is €10 each. Vulnerable households keep the €4 standing charge, start at €1 and do not go above €2 even past 61 cubic metres. The paper puts in that category people on the guaranteed minimum income or public assistance, recipients of the severe mobility allowance, pensioners over 70 on a low income, people with paraplegia or quadriplegia, recipients of the blindness grant, and large families or five-member families who receive child benefit.

The commercial scale is a €35 standing charge and about €1.20 to €1.50 per cubic metre depending on volume. Hotels pay €200 standing and €1.55 to €2.40. Stadiums, green spaces and livestock pay €4 standing and €1.35 per cubic metre. The paper puts a connection at €25, installation at a cap of €700, annual maintenance at up to €30, a meter check at €50, reconnection at €35 in ordinary hours and €70 outside them, and disconnection at €35.

For new development the charge on the land to be used is proposed at 1.2 per cent of the general valuation, plus a building-area fee: €1 per square metre up to 200, €2 from 201 to 450, €3 from 451 to 1,950 and €4 above 1,951. At Akrotiri, in the Kourion municipal district, the first band (1–10 cubic metres) would be €0.20 until 30 June 2029, then €0.90 until 30 June 2031, and only then the island-wide scale. In more than 100 mountain and remote villages in Nicosia, Larnaca, Limassol and Paphos that are supplied by government water works, consumption can be billed at a discount of up to 50 per cent.

The Legal Service has already looked at the regulations, the paper says. The attorney general said the power of an organisation’s board to delegate to the director general, and then to staff, needed a firmer place in statute. A bill titled the District Organisations of Local Government (Amendment) Law of 2026 was drafted alongside the regulations. Until the cabinet votes and the House approves the texts, the scale is not in force.
