A strike on Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery and tanker attacks in Hormuz lift oil

A projectile hit the Jazan refinery on Thursday, killing a Nepalese worker and halting some operations. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two tankers struck mines off Khasab and caught fire. Brent was at $96.61 on Friday morning, its highest since 6 November.

CyNews 24 Editorial TeamPublished: 27 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)Updated: 27 November 2026, 09:00 (EET)

Oil edged higher on Friday and is set to break a three-week fall, as attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and on Saudi energy facilities revived fears for supply from the Middle East. Reuters reported that by 04:50 GMT Brent was up 4 cents at $96.61 a barrel. On Thursday it gained nearly $2 and settled at its highest since 6 November. US West Texas Intermediate was at $85.78, up $2.10, or 2.5 per cent, from Wednesday's close. There was no WTI settlement on Thursday because of Thanksgiving.

Saudi Arabia's energy ministry said a hostile projectile struck the Jazan refinery, a Saudi Aramco plant, on Thursday. A Nepalese worker was killed, several people were wounded, and some operations were halted for a time. Reuters describes it as a fresh Houthi blow against Saudi facilities, a day after the group claimed attacks on Ras Tanura, the kingdom's largest refinery and its main crude-export hub, and on the Red Sea ports of Jeddah and Jazan.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps navy, in Anadolu's account, said two oil tankers hit naval mines and caught fire north of Oman's Khasab port while trying to cross the strait "in secret". Separately, the Saudi-led coalition that backs Yemen's recognised government said it had struck the Houthis and destroyed 50 military targets in Sanaa, Hudaydah and Saada provinces. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki called them legitimate military objectives and did not say what the targets were.

Analysts quoted by Reuters differ on how far supply is already broken. ANZ wrote that Brent can still push above $100 without another major escalation, but that this now needs evidence that deliverable oil is actually short. OCBC said recovering Gulf exports and an easing of geopolitical risk support lower prices in 2027, while depleted stocks, shipping risk and slow refinery repairs leave the market exposed. Two Reuters sources said OPEC+ is likely to keep its output targets at least through the first quarter of 2027 when it meets on Sunday, with the Iran war already disrupting supply. Prices had slipped in earlier weeks on relief that Donald Trump did not step up military action after the midterm elections. The strikes on ships in the strait and on Saudi infrastructure have taken that relief away.
